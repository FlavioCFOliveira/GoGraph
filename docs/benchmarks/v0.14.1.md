# Benchmarks — v0.14.1

The per-release measured record for `v0.14.1`, taken against `v0.14.0` first-hand. Nothing
here is carried forward from the `v0.14.0` campaign, and nothing here is derived from a
figure taken during development: the `v0.14.0` tree was extracted and **re-measured from
scratch**, in the same rounds, on the same host, against the same noise floor.

**Read [§1](#1-the-noise-floor--measured-first-before-any-delta-was-attributed) before any
number below.** Nothing in this document was attributed before that section's figures
existed, and the adjudication bars it derives are what separate a finding from noise on this
host. They are properties of **this** host and do not transfer.

**Headline: nothing a consumer runs got slower in a way this campaign can resolve, and the
release gives back the largest regression `v0.14.0` published.** Of **229 comparable result
rows**, 25 reached `p<0.05` and **11 survive adjudication against a same-code floor** —
**8 improvements and 3 regressions**, the largest regression **+6.54 %** on one 78 µs
benchmark. The Cypher read path is **unchanged at all five published concurrency levels**,
and the durable commit path is unchanged at all four it publishes. The costs are in
allocations, not in time, and they are itemised rather than averaged away.

## The arms

| Arm | Tree | Commit | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | `git archive` of tag `v0.14.0` | `7c59a02c` | base |
| `B` | `git archive` of `release/0.14.1` at `HEAD` | `efd32fb9` | head |
| `B2` | a **second, independent compilation of the same `v0.14.1` tree** | `efd32fb9` | **noise floor** |

The arms are extracted with `git archive` rather than checked out as git worktrees. That is
deliberate: the measurement then writes nothing into the repository's git administrative
area, and there is no worktree left behind to forget. Each tree is extracted under a
`.noindex` directory so Spotlight does not index it and contaminate the host — it did
exactly that during an earlier campaign on this machine.

`go build` is deterministic here under `-trimpath`, so `B2` comes out **byte-identical to
`B`** — verified by sha256 for **all 18** packages
([`binary-sha256.txt`](v0.14.1-raw/binary-sha256.txt)). `B` vs `B2` is therefore the same
binary measured against itself, **in the same rounds, in the same interleaving, under the
same load** as the signal it is the yardstick for. That is the only floor construction that
cannot be accused of having been measured under different conditions from the effect.

### Method

- **Three arms, interleaved arm-by-arm within each block** — never all of A then all of B.
  The arm order is **rotated every round** (`A B B2` / `B B2 A` / `B2 A B`), so no arm
  systematically inherits another's cache and thermal aftermath.
- **Six rounds**, giving `n=6` per arm — the minimum `benchstat` will compute a confidence
  interval from, and the minimum at which its permutation test can reach `p=0.002`.
- **A load gate before each round**, recorded with the value it opened at, bounded at 600 s
  so a busy host delays the round rather than stalling the campaign.
- **`uptime` bracketed before and after every single invocation.**
- **Exit codes appended to `exits.log` and read from there**, never from a pipeline's
  status.
- **stderr to its own file per invocation** — never `2>&1`.
- **Every kept line validated** against `^Benchmark\S*\s+\d+\s+`, with the arms' benchmark
  sets and per-benchmark sample counts asserted equal before any comparison is read.

**The load rises monotonically through a round, and it is the benchmarks' own load.** A
block that runs late in a round inherits the whole round's accumulated work, and `Lreadtx`
and `Ltxn` legitimately drive this 10-core host past 25 — a `b.RunParallel` block at

arm is compared only against siblings that ran under the same accumulated load, never against
an arm measured at a different point in the round.

**Residual host load was not zero and is not claimed to be.** iTerm2, a persistent
`osascript`, `WindowServer` and `btop` held roughly 0.3–0.5 of one core throughout, and the
per-round gate recorded the hot process list each time.

### One contamination event, and the measurement that settles it

At the start of round 4 the sampler and the hot-process census caught **Spotlight
(`mdworker_shared`) at 75.7 % CPU** — provoked by this harness's own per-round mirror of the
raw files into the repository, which Spotlight then indexed. Round 4's arm order is
`A B B2`, so the burst fell on arm A first and decayed across its siblings: a *systematically
unequal* load, which is the one thing interleaving cannot absorb.

**It did not bias the result, and that is measured rather than argued.** Taking the block's
most sensitive benchmark and printing its six per-round samples in order:

| arm | round 1 | 2 | 3 | **4** | 5 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `A` (`v0.14.0`) | 11.66 µs | 11.60 | 11.48 | **11.62** | 11.41 | 11.60 |
| `B` (`v0.14.1`) | 10.93 µs | 10.99 | 10.98 | **10.92** | 11.00 | 10.97 |
| `B2` | 10.87 µs | 10.90 | 10.89 | **10.98** | 11.02 | 10.86 |

Round 4's arm-A sample, 11.62 µs, sits in the middle of arm A's own range (11.41–11.66) and
is indistinguishable from its other five. The gate had already opened, and the burst decayed
before the timing began. The table also shows what the campaign is capable of resolving: `B`
and `B2` agree to **0.4 %** across six rounds while `A` is separated from both by **5.4 %**,
in every round.

The mirror directory was excluded from Spotlight (`.metadata_never_index`) after round 4 and
no further burst appeared. **A future campaign should mirror to a non-indexed path**; the
harness provoked this itself.

## 1. The noise floor — measured first, before any delta was attributed

**Nothing in this document was attributed before this section's numbers existed.**

### 1.1 Floor 1 — the same binary against itself, in the same rounds

`B` vs `B2`, byte-identical binaries (sha256-verified for all 18 packages), all 11 blocks
pooled, `n=6` per arm.

| Metric | Comparisons | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | significant at p<0.05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sec/op` | 243 | **0.49 %** | 1.95 % | **26.13 %** | **17** |
| `allocs/op` | 243 | **0.00 %** | 0.00 % | 0.14 % | 2 |

**Seventeen rows reached `p<0.05` from a binary that cannot have changed.** Sixteen of them
are between 0.62 % and 1.89 %. The seventeenth is
`BenchmarkReadTx_LockFree-1024`, **4.798 µs → 3.545 µs, −26.13 % at `p=0.0202`**, whose own
within-arm spread is ±28.6 % and ±24.5 %. Significance alone therefore establishes nothing
here, which is the entire reason §1.4 exists.

**`allocs/op` is all but exact** — 0.00 % at the median *and* the p95, with a maximum drift
of 0.14 %. Any allocation delta in §4 is real.

### 1.2 Floor 2 — an identical binary compared *across* the arms

`graph/index/count` compiles to a byte-identical test binary at both releases, so `A` vs `B`
on it is noise by construction — and it catches what a within-arm floor structurally cannot,
because it includes whatever differs between running arm A's binary and arm B's.

| Metric | Comparisons | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sec/op` | 9 | 1.05 % | 2.63 % | **7.87 %** | **0** |
| `allocs/op` | 9 | 0.00 % | 0.00 % | 0.00 % | 0 |

**Its n is 9, and that is a real limitation of this campaign.** `v0.14.0` had four
byte-identical packages and 55 cross-arm comparisons; this release changed `store/wal`, which
was one of them, so only one such package remains. Floor 2 is used here as a **cross-check on
Floor 1, not as a substitute for it**. Its largest drift, `BenchmarkStoreFootprint`
13.41 µs → 14.47 µs (+7.87 %, `p=0.109`), is a single non-significant point.

### 1.3 The floor is scale-dependent AND kind-dependent

Pooling hides two different shapes. Stratifying only by magnitude produced a **26.13 % bar**
for the 100 ns–10 µs band, driven entirely by that one 1024-goroutine cell — a bar that would
have rejected every real finding in the band. So the ladder cells at 64, 256 and 1024
goroutines are treated as their own population: on a 10-core host they measure inverse
*aggregate* throughput under oversubscription, where the scheduler's own variance dominates.

**Floor 1, serial and low-concurrency benchmarks (n=237)**

| Magnitude band | n | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | worst significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < 100 ns | 34 | 0.55 % | 1.95 % | 6.28 % | 1.36 % |
| 100 ns – 10 µs | 55 | 0.46 % | 1.79 % | 4.08 % | 1.89 % |
| 10 µs – 1 ms | 64 | 0.52 % | 2.59 % | 4.62 % | 1.36 % |
| > 1 ms | 84 | 0.46 % | 1.17 % | **1.68 %** | 1.28 % |

**Floor 1, oversubscribed ladder cells (n=6)**

| Magnitude band | n | median \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | worst significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 ns – 10 µs | 3 | 10.68 % | **26.13 %** | **26.13 %** |
| 10 µs – 1 ms | 3 | 0.75 % | 1.34 % | none |

**Millisecond-scale serial benchmarks are where this host is trustworthy** — a same-code
maximum of 1.68 % over 84 comparisons. **The oversubscribed cells below 10 µs are where it is
not**, and no directional claim is made about them.

### 1.4 The adjudication rule

A delta is reported as a **finding** only when it satisfies **both**:

1. `p < 0.05` (Mann-Whitney, `n=6` per arm); **and**
2. `|Δ|` exceeds the same-code envelope for its **magnitude band and benchmark kind** — the
   larger of that population's p95 same-code drift and the worst same-code drift that
   reached `p<0.05`, taken across both floors.

| Population | Band | Bar |
|---|---|---:|
| serial | < 100 ns | **1.95 %** |
| serial | 100 ns – 10 µs | **2.13 %** |
| serial | 10 µs – 1 ms | **7.87 %** |
| serial | > 1 ms | **1.28 %** |
| oversubscribed | 100 ns – 10 µs | **26.13 %** |
| oversubscribed | 10 µs – 1 ms | **0.75 %** |

**The 10 µs – 1 ms bar is the weak one and is flagged rather than hidden.** It comes from
Floor 2's single point, where Floor 1's 64 serial comparisons give 2.59 %. Anything between
2.59 % and 7.87 % is therefore reported as **inconclusive** — neither a finding nor
dismissed as noise.

**25 rows reached `p<0.05`. 11 are findings, 5 are inconclusive, 9 are rejected as noise.**
Full table: [`v0.14.1-raw/adjudication.txt`](v0.14.1-raw/adjudication.txt).

## 3. The concurrency ladders the README publishes

### 3.1 The Cypher read transaction — the release-level concurrency result

This is the path `v0.14.0` added counting to and `v0.14.1` added an index-seek guard to, so
it is the one that matters most.

| Goroutines | 1 | 8 | 64 | 256 | 1024 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ReadTx_LockFree` `v0.14.0` → `v0.14.1` | 3.053 → 3.040 µs | 1.653 → 1.653 µs | 2.149 → 2.236 µs | 2.719 → 2.823 µs | 4.619 → 4.798 µs |
| `ReadTx_WriterLock` `v0.14.0` → `v0.14.1` | 101.0 → 101.6 µs | 24.78 → 24.75 µs | 23.75 → 23.99 µs | 24.84 → 25.10 µs | 28.35 → 29.49 µs |

**Not one of the ten cells is significant** — the smallest `p` is 0.066, and the block's
same-binary floor geomean is **−4.57 %** against an effect geomean of +1.78 %: the floor is
larger than the signal. **The Cypher read path is unchanged at all five published
concurrency levels.**

The 64/256/1024 cells carry a same-code drift of up to **−26.13 %** in this very campaign, so
the apparent +3.8 to +4.1 % at those levels is **not** a result and is not reported as one.

`ns/op` under `RunParallel` is inverse *aggregate* throughput, not per-goroutine latency.

**Allocations did move here, and this is the release's clearest measured cost.**

| Benchmark | `allocs/op` | `B/op` | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ReadTx_WriterLock`, **every level** | **114 → 116 (+2)** | **48 281 → 48 633 (+352, +0.73 %)** | every sample equal in both arms |
| `ReadTx_LockFree`, every level | unchanged | +0.06 % to +0.89 % | samples vary |

The `WriterLock` figure is identical at 1, 8, 64, 256 and 1024 goroutines with **every sample
equal**, which is what a per-transaction constant looks like. The plausible attribution is
#2814's plan-footprint guard, whose own task-level measurement was +360 B and +8 allocs on a
`SET` statement — the same order of bytes. **Attribution is a hypothesis, not established**:
no third arm with the guard disabled was built.

`store/txn` `BenchmarkCommitConcurrent`, median of 6, re-measured at both trees.

| Writers | 1 | 8 | 64 | 256 | 1024 |

## 4. The findings

**11 of 229 comparable rows survive adjudication: 8 improvements and 3 regressions.**

### 4.1 The ORDER BY key hoist pays, and the whole family moves together

#2662 hoists a non-projected `ORDER BY` key into its own hidden column so the projection no

| `BoundedOrder/n=000110/Sort` | 8.487 ms | 8.373 ms | **−1.34 %** | 0.045 | −0.64 % |
| `n=000010/Sort`, `n=010010/Sort`, `n=060000/Sort`, `n=120000/Sort`, `n=120000/Top` | | | −1.24 % to −2.06 % | 0.066–0.230 | — |

### 4.2 `v0.14.1` gives back the largest regression `v0.14.0` published

`AllNodesScan_PerNodeAllocCost`: **11.60 µs → 10.98 µs, −5.39 % (`p=0.005`)**.

`v0.14.0`'s own report named this benchmark as **its largest regression, +7.11 %**, when
per-node db-hits counting was added to exactly this path. This campaign measures it back down,
and the per-round table in the campaign section shows the separation is systematic: `B` and
`B2` agree to 0.4 % in every one of six rounds while `A` is 5.4 % above both, in every one.

It is classified **inconclusive** by the conservative 7.87 % bar and a **finding** by Floor 1's
2.59 %. Both readings are given rather than one being chosen.

**The regression is not systemic across the expansion machinery, and that is the useful

parity. Only the OUT single-source per-edge path and the IN deg1-sources baseline move.

## 5. The checkpoint readback — priced, with no baseline to compare against

The five `store/checkpoint` benchmarks are **new in this window**: `v0.14.0` has none, so
there is nothing to compare and none is claimed. What they do give is a decomposition of what
#2749 and #2780 cost, measured *within* the release, which is a better answer than an A/B
could have given.

| Benchmark | `v0.14.1` median of 6 | same-binary floor |
|---|---:|---:|
| `Checkpoint_FullRunWithReadback` — the denominator | **150.3 ms** | −0.57 % |
| `Checkpoint_SnapshotReadbackOnly` — #2749's added cost alone | **14.73 ms** | −0.53 % |
| `Checkpoint_CodecReadback_ParseOnly` — the #2749 half on a v2 image | 14.24 ms | −0.40 % |
| `Checkpoint_CodecReadback_ParseAndDecode` — the shipped readback | 14.70 ms | −0.36 % |
| `Checkpoint_MapperDecodeOnly` — #2780's decode, no filesystem call | **235.6 µs** | −2.59 % |

**Reading the snapshot back costs 14.73 ms against a 150.3 ms checkpoint — 9.8 %.** Within
that, **#2780's decode pass adds 0.46 ms** (14.70 − 14.24), which is 3.1 % of the readback and
**0.31 % of a full checkpoint**. `MapperDecodeOnly` at 235.6 µs carries no filesystem call at
all, which is the direct evidence for the "zero extra I/O" half of the #2780 claim.

That is what the durability fix costs: **a tenth of a checkpoint, to stop the WAL prefix
being discarded against a snapshot nothing can read.**

## 7. Reproduce

All three arms are pre-compiled once, before any timing, and each arm runs its own binary.
Every script is in [`v0.14.1-raw/`](v0.14.1-raw/) and was used verbatim.

```bash
# 1. Three trees, extracted with `git archive` rather than `git worktree`, so the
#    measurement writes nothing into .git — and under a .noindex directory so
#    Spotlight does not index them and contaminate the host (it did exactly that
#    in an earlier campaign).
git archive --format=tar v0.14.0 | tar -x -C <scratch>/bench.noindex/A
git archive --format=tar HEAD   | tar -x -C <scratch>/bench.noindex/B
git archive --format=tar HEAD   | tar -x -C <scratch>/bench.noindex/B2

# 2. Build A, B and B2. -trimpath so the tree path is not baked in and a binary's
#    sha256 becomes real evidence about the CODE.
bash build.sh                          # 54 builds; every exit code in build-exits.log
shasum -a 256 <scratch>/bin/*.test > binary-sha256.txt
#   assert B == B2 for every package  -> the within-arm floor is valid
#   note which A == B                 -> those are the cross-arm floors

# 3. Size the campaign from a MEASURED cost, never a guessed one.
bash size.sh                           # one invocation of every block on arm B, timed

# 4. The campaign: interleaved, arm order rotated every round, load-gated per round,
#    uptime bracketed per invocation, stderr to its own file, exits to exits.log.
ROUNDS=6 GATE=2.5 nohup bash campaign.sh &

# 5. Integrity FIRST, then floors, then bands, then adjudication, then effects.
#    In that order, and the script enforces the order.
ROUNDS=6 python3 report.py integrity
ROUNDS=6 python3 report.py floor
ROUNDS=6 python3 report.py adjudicate
ROUNDS=6 python3 report.py effects <block>...

# 6. Canonical statistics, per block.
benchstat "v0.14.0=<c>/<blk>_A.txt" "v0.14.1=<c>/<blk>_B.txt"   # effect
benchstat "B=<c>/<blk>_B.txt"       "B2=<c>/<blk>_B2.txt"       # floor

# 7. The exported-surface diff, which is evidence for the semver classification
#    rather than for performance.
python3 apidiff.py   <scratch>/bench.noindex/A <scratch>/bench.noindex/B
python3 fielddiff.py <scratch>/bench.noindex/A <scratch>/bench.noindex/B
bash godocdiff.sh
```

**Validate before you believe any comparison.** Assert that every kept line matches
`^Benchmark\S*\s+\d+\s+`; a log line landing mid-result silently removes samples and
`benchstat` will not tell you. Count the surviving result lines against the number expected,
per arm, per block.

**Read every exit status from inside the log, never from a wrapper.** A `… | tail` pipeline
reports `tail`'s status, and a real `Error 1` has been masked that way on this project
before. `exits.log` is written by the runner itself, one line per invocation.

## 8. What this snapshot does NOT measure

`v0.14.1` is a patch release of 50 commits that are overwhelmingly bug fixes, and the
campaign was cut to match it: **11 blocks, not 23**. Every block that was sized and then
dropped is listed here with the reason, so a reader can tell a deliberate omission from an
oversight.

**Dropped on cost, after being sized:**

| Dropped | Why |
|---|---|
| the full 149-benchmark `cypher` sweep (kept 60) | 454 s per arm against 204 s for the targeted set. At 3 arms × 6 rounds the full package alone is 2.3 hours, and the benchmarks omitted price paths no commit in this window touched. **`cypher` is sampled, not covered — 60 of 149 functions, 40 %.** |
| `graph/index`, `graph/index/btree`, `graph/index/hash` | Changed, but their benchmarks price data-structure primitives. The index defects this release fixes were **correctness** defects (#2778, #2792, #2793, #2799); the guards' cost is measured at task level and in the `exec` and `cy` blocks. |
| `graph/lpg` | Changed (`readview.go`, `mvcc_index.go`). Its 33 benchmarks include the `ReadScale1671_*` family that `v0.14.0` also skipped on cost. #2775's own **+3.58 %** figure is quoted at task level in the release notes instead, and is **not** re-measured here. |
| `store/snapshot`, `store/wal` | Changed additively only — a manifest field and a godoc. |
| six further concurrency ladders (`Lcypar`, `Llpg`, `Lbolt`, `Lmvcc`, `Lprom`, `Lmapper`) | Five `-test.cpu` levels each. The README publishes two ladders; those two are measured. |

**Not measured at all, and unchanged from the previous release's gaps:**

1. **Latency percentiles at the published concurrency levels.** Everything here is per-op
   cost or aggregate throughput, never a distribution. **Eighth consecutive cycle.**
2. **1 024 concurrent writers on the durable path.** `BenchmarkCommitConcurrent` stops at 256.
3. **On-disk footprint growth.** No benchmark reporting bytes written to disk exists in both
   trees.
4. **`bench/mtaudit`'s engine-under-writer ladder** — `v0.14.0` called this "the single
   highest-value measurement not made, now for two consecutive releases". It is now three.
5. **The soak and nightly layers**, and any production certification.
6. **No `-race` figures and no profiles.** Every allocation number here is a non-race number
   and must not be compared against one taken under `-race`.
7. **Nothing here is a comparison against Neo4j, Memgraph or any other engine.**

**Two limitations specific to this campaign:**

8. **Floor 2 rests on nine comparisons**, because `store/wal` changed and stopped being a
   byte-identical package. `v0.14.0` had 55. The 10 µs – 1 ms bar therefore comes from a
   single point, and §1.4 reports the affected rows as inconclusive rather than resolving
   them.
9. **Attribution is established for nothing.** No third arm was built with
   `DisableIndexSeek` or `DisableExpandLabelPush` set, so every cause named in §4 is a
   hypothesis consistent with the diff, not a measured attribution.

10. **One host, one architecture** — Apple M4, 10 cores, `darwin/arm64`, macOS 26.6.2. The
    floors in §1 are properties of **this host**, and the adjudication bars derived from them
    do not transfer. Note the host OS differs from `v0.14.0`'s (26.5.2), which is one reason
    that campaign's floors were not reused and its `v0.14.0` arm was re-measured here rather
    than quoted.
