# Benchmarks — v0.14.0

Headline benchmark and load-test numbers for the GoGraph **v0.14.0** release, measured
first-hand as a **direct, interleaved A/B against the `v0.13.0` tree on this host**.

> **What moved in v0.14.0.** One thing enormously and conditionally; almost nothing else.
>
> **Sprint 355 was an execution-reporting sprint.** It added counting work — db-hits,
> rows-removed, per-operator estimates — to the Cypher read path, to four operator
> families, to the parallel leaves and to the planner. The release's open question was
> what that added work *costs*. Its own tasks answered that in allocations only, each
> declining a timing claim because the host was not quiet
> ([release-notes/v0.14.0.md](../../release-notes/v0.14.0.md), "Performance — what was
> added, and what is not yet measured"). **This document is the timing answer, and the
> answer is that the added work costs nothing this campaign can measure.** 595 benchmark
> functions are common to both trees; this campaign exercised a selection of them across
> 14 blocks, yielding **272 comparable result rows**, each measured at `-count=6` on
> **three** arms. **Nine** deltas survive adjudication against a same-code noise floor. Six are
> improvements, three are regressions, and the largest regression is **+7.11 %** on one
> 11 µs benchmark.
>
> **The one large gain: the join reorder.** On the skewed two-component shape the
> property-statistics reorder (rmp #2766) takes **267.155 ms → 2.723 ms, −98.98 %**
> (**98.1×**), `p=0.002`, `n=6`, with `B/op` **47 226.6 KiB → 553.4 KiB (−98.83 %)** and
> `allocs/op` **5 925 706 → 59 968 (−98.99 %)**. Its two controls — the same shape with
> `reorder=off`, and the drain-cost harness — are **unchanged** (`~`, `p=0.589` and
> `p=0.180`/`p=0.699`), and the plan itself changes shape between the trees, so the
> attribution is not resting on the numbers alone. **The gain is conditional** and must
> never be quoted as a general speed-up: see §5.
>
> **What a consumer actually feels: the Cypher read path is unchanged, and that is the
> result.** `ReadTx_LockFree` and `ReadTx_WriterLock` show **no significant change at any
> of the five published concurrency levels** (§3.1), and their absolute figures reproduce
> `v0.13.0`'s published head arm to within a few percent. The −73 % geomean that
> `v0.13.0` won on this path is **held**, not eroded, by a release that added counting
> work to it.
>
> **The largest regression** is `cypher/exec` `AllNodesScan_PerNodeAllocCost`,
> **10.97 µs → 11.75 µs, +7.11 %**, `p=0.0051` (§6.1) — a benchmark whose entire purpose
> is to price the per-node scan path, on a release that added per-node counting to it.
>
> **The methodological result, which outranks several of the above.** Two independent
> same-code floors were measured in the same rounds as the signal. They show that on this
> host a **statistically significant** cross-arm difference of **−6.21 %** is produced by
> a **byte-identical binary**, and that at nanosecond scale under parallelism the same
> binary can differ by **+37.80 %** and report a completely different scaling curve
> (**5.32× vs 3.83×**). Eighteen of the 27 rows that reached `p<0.05` are therefore
> reported as **noise, not findings** — including four on a binary that cannot have
> changed (§1.4). A p-value is not evidence on this machine; a p-value that clears its
> own band's demonstrated same-code envelope is.

> Re-measure on your own hardware before relying on any absolute number; these are
> medians of interleaved A/B/B2 runs from one documented session, not a guarantee.

## Its relation to the allocation-only comparison

[`v0130-vs-v0140-2026-09-06.md`](v0130-vs-v0140-2026-09-06.md) already compared these two
trees in **one dimension — allocations** — and deliberately made **no timing claim**,
because the host then carried a 1-minute load average of **2.86–19.73**: Spotlight was
indexing the two 1.9 GiB worktrees that experiment had itself created.

**That document is not superseded and is not restated here.** It found 159 of 171 compared
benchmarks unchanged, 8 reduced, 0 regressed. This document adds the dimension it could
not carry, on a quiet host, and **its allocation findings are independently reproduced
here** at `-count=6` (§4.3): the same shortest-path reduction (126 → 108), the same
reorder collapse, and the same conclusion that the plan-cache shapes are too noisy to
carry an allocation claim.

**Why the host is quiet this time.** The worktrees were created under a directory whose
name ends in **`.noindex`**, which Spotlight skips. `mds_stores` did not appear in the CPU
top at any point during the campaign.

## The arms, and the confound that is absent

`go.mod` is byte-identical between the two trees, and both arms were compiled by the
**same local toolchain, go1.27.1**, from their own clean worktrees, and run **interleaved
on the same host in the same session**.

| Arm | Tree | Commit | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | detached worktree of tag `v0.13.0` | `b439283e` | base |
| `B` | detached worktree of `release/0.14.0` | `f8a3ac0b` | head |
| `B2` | **a second, independent build of the same `v0.14.0` tree** | `f8a3ac0b` | **noise floor** |

**`B2` is the reason this document can say anything at all.** Go's build is deterministic
here under `-trimpath`, so `B2` came out **byte-identical to `B`** — verified by sha256 on
all twelve packages. `B` vs `B2` is therefore *the same binary against itself*, measured
**in the same rounds, in the same interleaving, at the same concurrency levels, under the
same load** as the signal it is the yardstick for.

**The head arm is `f8a3ac0b`, not `94602f46`.** The allocation comparison used
`94602f46` plus one uncommitted working-tree change. The only Go file that differs between
those two commits is `cypher/api.go`, and the difference is **a doc comment** — verified
by `git diff`. The two arms are therefore the same code, and this document's `B` is a
clean commit rather than a commit plus a patch.

### Method

- **Three arms, interleaved arm-by-arm within each block** — never all of A then all of B.
  The arm order is **rotated every round** (`A B B2` / `B B2 A` / `B2 A B`), so no arm
  systematically inherits another's cache and thermal aftermath.
- **Six rounds**, giving `n=6` per arm, which is the minimum `benchstat` will compute a
  confidence interval from and the minimum at which its permutation test can reach
  `p=0.002`.
- **A load gate before each round**, recorded with the value it opened at.
- **`uptime` bracketed before and after every single invocation** — all 252 of them.
- **Exit codes appended to `exits.log` and read from there**, never from a pipeline's
  status.
- **stderr to its own file per invocation** — never `2>&1`.
- **Every kept line validated** against `^Benchmark\S*\s+\d+\s+`, with the three arms'
  benchmark sets and sample counts asserted equal.

## 1. The noise floor — measured first, before any delta was attributed

**Nothing in this document was attributed before this section's numbers existed.**

### 1.1 Floor 1 — the same binary against itself, in the same rounds

`B` vs `B2`, byte-identical binaries, all 14 blocks pooled, `n=6` per arm.

| Metric | Comparisons | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | significant at p<0.05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sec/op` | 272 | **0.62 %** | 3.40 % | **30.19 %** | **4** |
| `allocs/op` | 272 | **0.00 %** | 0.00 % | 7.95 % | **0** |

The four "significant" `sec/op` rows are **−2.40 %, −0.62 %, −0.60 % and +0.59 %** — from
a binary that cannot have changed. The largest single drift is
`Scan_PerNode/N=4096`, **7.125 µs → 4.974 µs, −30.19 %** (`p=0.066`), a benchmark whose
own within-arm spread is ±38–45 %.

The largest `allocs/op` drift is `PlanCacheHit_Parameterised`, **88 → 95, +7.95 %**
(`p=0.618`, not significant) — the same plan-cache shape the allocation comparison
independently flagged as unreliable, at a comparable magnitude (it saw 11.34 %).

### 1.2 Floor 2 — an identical binary compared *across* the arms

`A` vs `B` on the five packages whose test binary is byte-identical between the releases.
Every number here is noise by construction, and it is the floor that actually governs a
cross-release claim.

| Metric | Comparisons | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | significant at p<0.05 | worst significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sec/op` | 55 | **0.40 %** | 4.66 % | **37.80 %** | **5** | **−6.21 %** |
| `allocs/op` | 55 | **0.00 %** | 0.00 % | **0.00 %** | **0** | — |

**Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions.**

- **`allocs/op` is exact across the arms — 0.00 % at the median, the p95 *and* the
  maximum.** Allocation counts are perfectly reproducible on this host under this harness,
  so **any** non-zero allocation delta in §4.3 is real. This is the strongest form of the
  claim the allocation comparison made, now measured across arms rather than within one.
- **`sec/op` is not.** A **statistically significant −6.21 %** was produced by a
  byte-identical binary. Significance alone therefore establishes nothing here.

### 1.3 The floor is scale-dependent, so the yardstick is too

Pooling hides the shape. Both floors, stratified by the magnitude of the benchmark itself:

**Floor 1 — same binary, within arm (272 comparisons)**

| Magnitude band | n | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | worst significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < 100 ns | 71 | 0.51 % | 2.59 % | 26.82 % | 0.62 % |
| 100 ns – 10 µs | 62 | 0.72 % | 4.28 % | 30.19 % | 0.60 % |
| 10 µs – 1 ms | 53 | 0.52 % | 2.89 % | 29.62 % | 2.40 % |
| > 1 ms | 86 | 0.68 % | 2.62 % | **4.10 %** | 0.59 % |

**Floor 2 — identical binary, across arms (55 comparisons)**

| Magnitude band | n | median \|Δ\| | p95 \|Δ\| | max \|Δ\| | worst significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < 100 ns | 36 | 0.42 % | 6.21 % | **37.80 %** | **6.21 %** |
| 100 ns – 10 µs | 11 | 0.49 % | 1.58 % | 1.86 % | none |
| 10 µs – 1 ms | 1 | 1.75 % | 1.75 % | 1.75 % | 1.75 % |
| > 1 ms | 7 | **0.17 %** | 0.42 % | **0.87 %** | none |

**Millisecond-scale benchmarks are where this host is trustworthy** — a cross-arm floor of
0.17 % at the median and 0.87 % at the maximum. **Nanosecond-scale benchmarks are where it
is not** — 37.80 % maximum and a *significant* 6.21 %.

### 1.4 The adjudication rule, and the proof that it is necessary

A delta is reported as a **finding** only when it satisfies **both**:

1. `p < 0.05` (Mann-Whitney, `n=6` per arm); **and**
2. `|Δ|` exceeds its own magnitude band's **same-code envelope** — the larger of that
   band's p95 same-code drift and the worst same-code drift that reached `p<0.05`, taken
   across both floors.

| Magnitude band | Envelope (the bar) |
|---|---:|
| < 100 ns | **6.21 %** |
| 100 ns – 10 µs | **4.28 %** |
| 10 µs – 1 ms | **2.89 %** |
| > 1 ms | **2.62 %** |

**27 rows reached `p<0.05`. Nine are findings. Eighteen are rejected as noise.**

**The rule is validated on ground truth, not asserted.** Four of the eighteen rejected rows
are in `store/wal` — **a binary that is byte-identical between the arms** — and they had
earned `p` values of 0.0082 to 0.0450 with effects of **−6.21 %, −4.66 %, −3.57 % and
+2.07 %**. They cannot be real. The rule rejects all four. A rule built on `p<0.05` alone
would have published them as findings, and this report would have opened with a fictitious
6 % CRC improvement.

Full adjudication, every significant row with its band and bar:
[`v0.14.0-raw/adjudication.txt`](v0.14.0-raw/adjudication.txt).

## 3. The concurrency ladder — 1 / 8 / 64 / 256 / 1024

CLAUDE.md's EXTREME/MASSIVE Concurrent Ready mandate publishes this ladder, so it is swept
with `-test.cpu=1,8,64,256,1024`, `-count=6` per arm.

**Read `ns/op` correctly under `b.RunParallel`:** `b.N` is the total iteration count across
all goroutines, so `ns/op` is **inverse aggregate throughput**, not per-goroutine latency.

**Scaling is reported as a ratio to each arm's own level-1 cell**, never as a raw
cross-level number.

Full tables: [`v0.14.0-raw/ladder.txt`](v0.14.0-raw/ladder.txt).

### 3.0 Which of these is actually a ladder — read this before any figure below

`-test.cpu` only builds a **goroutine** ladder for a benchmark that creates concurrency
**itself**. On a serial benchmark it varies `GOMAXPROCS` and nothing else: no concurrent
reader, no contention, no scaling curve.

`v0.13.0`'s report found that **four** of the benchmarks its ladder originally named did
not measure concurrency at all. That classification was **not inherited**. Every benchmark
function in both trees was re-parsed here for two independent signals — a `b.RunParallel`
call, **and** a hand-rolled `go func` fan-out behind a `WaitGroup` — because either check
alone misfiles something:

- **A name is not evidence.** `graph/lpg`'s `BenchmarkOutDegreeByType_Parallel` is serial.
- **Absence of `RunParallel` is not evidence of serial.** `store/txn`'s
  `BenchmarkCommitConcurrent` drives genuinely concurrent commits behind a `WaitGroup` and
  a `RunParallel`-only screen would have misfiled it.

**Result: 595 benchmark functions are common to both trees; 10 were added by `v0.14.0`
(all of them instruments); 57 are genuinely concurrent in both trees; and 0 are classified
differently between the trees.** Every ladder block below was drawn from those 57.
Classification: [`v0.14.0-raw/concurrency-classification.txt`](v0.14.0-raw/concurrency-classification.txt).

agree — which is a check on both.

## 5. The join reorder — the release's one large gain, and its conditions

This is the only place in the release where the numbers are large, and it is the place
where the caveats matter most.

**Measured head-to-head, which the release notes said could not be done.** The sprint's own
`BenchmarkJoinReorderStatsSkewed` cannot compile against `v0.13.0` — not because the engine

already existed there.

**Why this is attribution and not correlation.** Three independent pieces of evidence, and
the numbers are the weakest of them:

1. **The plan changes shape.** Dumped from both trees by an identical test
   ([`plandump-v0130.txt`](v0130-vs-v0140-2026-09-06-raw/plandump-v0130.txt),
   [`plandump-v0140.txt`](v0130-vs-v0140-2026-09-06-raw/plandump-v0140.txt)): at `v0.13.0`
   the 100-row `:B` component drives and re-scans the 20 000-node `:A` label once per row;
   at `v0.14.0` the `(label, property)` statistics estimate the filtered `:A` component at
   **3 rows**, so it drives instead. That mechanism *predicts* a ~100× drop, and a ~100×
   drop is what was measured.
2. **The controls are flat.** The same shape with the peephole disabled is unchanged at
   `p=0.589` on time and `p=0.394` on bytes. The harness is not the cause.
3. **The result is preserved.** The full sorted result set from both trees hashes to the
   same md5, `n=300` rows on both. The reorder is result-preserving.

### The conditions, without which the figure is meaningless

**This is not a general speed-up and must never be quoted as one.** It requires **all** of:

- **`RefreshStatistics` must have run.** Without populated `(label, property)` statistics
  the gate has nothing to read and the peephole does not fire.
- **A qualifying query shape** — a disjoint two-component match in which a label count
  alone cannot identify the cheaper driver, but a `(label, property)` estimate can. Here
  that is one synthetic skewed shape chosen to exhibit the effect: 20 000 `:A` nodes of
  which 3 satisfy the predicate, against 100 `:B` nodes. `:A` is the **larger** label, so
  a count-only gate picks the wrong driver by construction.
- **The trustworthiness veto must not fire.** The gate reads two estimate sources and
  vetoes both when either is untrustworthy.

On a query that does not meet those conditions the peephole does not fire and the cost is
what `reorder=off` measures — **unchanged**. A workload of ordinary queries will see
**none** of this 98 %.

**What is not separated.** Both `history=quiet` and `history=live` gain ~96 %. The plan
dumps show the reorder fires in neither arm at `v0.13.0` and in both at `v0.14.0`. The
quiet-arm gain is attributable to **#2766**; the live-arm gain requires **#2766 and #2771
together**, and because no build carrying #2766 *without* #2771 was produced, **#2771's
isolated contribution is not separated by this experiment.** What is shown is that the
gain **survives live MVCC history**, which is what #2771 exists to guarantee.

**Consistency with the per-task measurement.** The release notes record this gain measured
at task level as `261.638 ms → 2.658 ms, −98.98 %, n=12`, on a host that was not quiet.
This campaign measures `267.155 ms → 2.723 ms, −98.98 %, n=6` on a quiet host with
interleaved arms and a 0.08 % same-code floor. **The two agree to the second decimal place
on the percentage**, and the absolute figures differ by ~2 %, which is within the
cross-session envelope. The per-task claim is confirmed.

**Three regressions survive adjudication.** All are `-race` **off**, and — importantly —

2. **1024 concurrent writers on the durable path.** `BenchmarkCommitConcurrent`'s own
   ladder stops at **256** (§3.4), so the published 1024 level is measured for **reads and
   metrics, not for durable commits**. Closing this needs a change to the benchmark, not to
   this campaign.
3. **43 of the 57 concurrent benchmarks common to both trees were not exercised.** Only 14
   were. The omissions, by size:
   - **`bolt/server` — 11**, including the whole `TxBookkeeping_*` family and
     `MsgObserve_RealBackendParallel`. `bolt/server/plan_meta.go` **changed by 103 lines in
     this release** and only its record-encode path was measured. This is the largest
     changed-surface gap in the document.
   - **`bench/mtaudit` — 2** (`EngReadUnderWriter`, `EngWriteAutocommit`): the engine's read
     and write paths under a concurrent writer, the closest instrument the module has to its
     published concurrency story. `v0.13.0` cut these on a **measured** ~12 min per
     arm-round; at `n=6` × 3 arms that is ~3.5 h. **It remains the single highest-value
     measurement not made**, now for two consecutive releases.
   - **`bench/mvccwrite` — 5**, `graph/index/btree` — 4, `graph/lpg` — 4
     (`ReadScale1671_View{Barrier,UnderWriter}`, `TombstoneScanClean`, `TombstoneScanTombstoned`
     — also cut by `v0.13.0`), `bench/cypher_ldbc` — 3 (`IC1/IC2/IC9_Parallel`, the flagship
     LDBC read suite under concurrency), `graph/index/{count,label}` — 3 each, `bench/soak` — 3,
     `bench/audit352` — 2, `cypher` — 1 (`ReaderLatencyUnderHeldWriteTx`),
     `graph/index/hash` — 1, `internal/metrics` — 1.
4. **`cypher` is sampled, not covered.** 62 of its **144** benchmark functions were
   exercised — **43 %**. The 82 not run include the planner and parser families, most of the
   `CREATE`/`MERGE`/`SET` write shapes, and most columnar shapes. `cypher/exec` **was**
   covered completely (all 36 functions, 62 result rows).
5. **Whole packages were not touched at all:** `graph/adjlist`, `graph/index/{btree,hash,label}`,
   `store/{snapshot,recovery,checkpoint}`, the rest of `bolt/`, `internal/sim`, and every
   `bench/*` harness. `internal/sim/access_parity.go` changed by 92 lines and is unmeasured.
6. **The soak and nightly layers were not run.** No `-tags=soak`, no `-tags=nightly`, no
   multi-hour mixed workload, no `GODEBUG=gctrace=1` growth check on heap, file descriptors
   or goroutines. CLAUDE.md makes soak a periodic exercise rather than a release gate, but
   **this document is not evidence about sustained-load behaviour** and must not be read as
   any.
7. **No `-race` figures, and no correctness gate was run here.** `make ci` was explicitly
   out of scope for this task, so this document says **nothing** about the openCypher TCK
   count, `goleak`, the race detector, or the crash/recovery battery. Those remain the
   release gate's job.
8. **No CPU or heap profiles, and no flame graphs.** None were captured, and none would have
   settled the attribution questions in §6 anyway: **a profile of one arm cannot attribute a
   difference between two arms.** Doing that needs a third build per hypothesis, and none was
   made.
9. **Attribution to individual commits is unestablished except for the reorder.** This
   campaign compared two trees separated by a whole sprint. §5's attribution rests on a plan
   dump and two flat controls; §6's candidates are named as **candidates**.
10. **On-disk storage footprint** (§7). No benchmark reporting disk bytes exists in both
    trees.
11. **Single host, single architecture** — Apple M4, 10 cores, `darwin/arm64`, macOS. No
    Linux, no NUMA, no multi-socket, no container. The floors in §1 are properties of **this
    host**, and the adjudication bars derived from them do not transfer.
12. **The 1024-goroutine cells are the weakest data in the document** even where reported.
    Within-arm spreads there reach ±94 %, and §3.6 shows an identical binary differing by
    38 % at 8 goroutines. Directions at 256 and 1024 should be treated as unresolved unless
    the effect is an order of magnitude.
13. **Nothing here is a comparison against Neo4j, Memgraph or any other engine.** The
    peer comparisons in `docs/benchmarks/` are separate documents and were not re-run.

## 9. Reproduce

All three arms are pre-compiled once, before any timing, and each arm runs its own binary.
Scripts are in [`v0.14.0-raw/`](v0.14.0-raw/) and were used verbatim.

```bash
# 1. Three clean worktrees' worth of arms, under a .noindex directory so Spotlight
#    does not index them and contaminate the host (it did exactly that last time).
git worktree add --detach <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt13 v0.13.0     # b439283e
git worktree add --detach <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt14 f8a3ac0b

# 2. Carry the instruments across, so the comparison is head-to-head rather than
#    one-sided. Both files end up byte-identical in both arms (verify with shasum).
cp <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt14/cypher/exec/shortest_path_bench_test.go \
   <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt13/cypher/exec/
cp zzport_reorder_bench_test.go.txt <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt13/cypher/zzport_reorder_bench_test.go
cp zzport_reorder_bench_test.go.txt <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt14/cypher/zzport_reorder_bench_test.go
( cd <scratch>/bench.noindex/wt13 && go vet ./cypher/ ./cypher/exec/ )   # exit 0

# 3. Build A, B and B2. -trimpath so the worktree path is not baked in and a
#    binary's sha256 becomes real evidence about the CODE.
bash build.sh                 # 33 builds + graph/index/count; all exit 0
shasum -a 256 <scratch>/bin/*.test > binary-sha256.txt
#   assert B == B2 for every package  -> the within-arm floor is valid
#   note which A == B                 -> those are the cross-arm floors

# 4. Size the campaign from a MEASURED cost, never a guessed one.
bash size.sh                  # one arm = 529 s => a 3-arm round = ~26.4 min

# 5. The campaign: 14 blocks x 3 arms x 6 rounds, interleaved, order rotated,
#    load-gated per round, uptime bracketed per invocation, stderr to its own file.
ROUNDS=6 GATE=2.5 nohup bash campaign.sh &     # 10:05:16Z -> 13:15:03Z

# 6. Integrity FIRST, then floors, then effects. In that order.
python3 report.py integrity    # 0 malformed, n=6 exactly, arms matched
python3 report.py floor        # Floor 1 (B vs B2) and Floor 2 (identical binaries)
python3 report.py              # per-benchmark effect beside its OWN floor

# 7. Canonical statistics.
benchstat "v0.13.0=<c>/<blk>_A.txt" "v0.14.0=<c>/<blk>_B.txt"   # effect
benchstat "B=<c>/<blk>_B.txt"       "B2=<c>/<blk>_B2.txt"       # floor

# 8. Footprint, at the DEFAULT GOMAXPROCS only (see §7 for why not the ladder).
bash footprint.sh

# Deliberately NOT run, and recorded so it is not silently skipped again:
#   ./bench/mtaudit  '^(BenchmarkEngReadUnderWriter|BenchmarkEngWriteAutocommit)$'
#       -> ~12 min per arm-round; ~3.5 h at n=6 x 3 arms. Highest-value gap (§8.3).
#   ./bolt/server    '^BenchmarkTxBookkeeping_'   -> 11 benchmarks, changed surface.
#   ./graph/lpg      '^(BenchmarkReadScale1671_View(Barrier|UnderWriter)|BenchmarkTombstoneScan)'
#   ./bench/cypher_ldbc '^BenchmarkIC[129]_Parallel$'
```

**Validate before you believe any comparison.** Assert that every kept line matches
`^Benchmark\S*\s+\d+\s+`; a log line landing mid-result silently removes samples and
`benchstat` will not tell you. Count surviving result lines against the number expected,
per arm. This campaign diverted **1 041** stderr lines that would otherwise have landed in
the data stream.

**Two harness defects were found and fixed before any figure was taken**, both by the
sizing run, which is why one exists:

- **The block table's field delimiter was `|`, and the benchmark selectors are regexes
  whose alternation is also `|`**, so `read` split the selector itself. The `cy` block
  exited **1** with **0** result lines. Fixed by delimiting on `#`.
- **The load gate could not fire.** This host's locale is `pt_PT`, so `uptime` prints
  `10,01`; the obvious shell gate (`tr ',' '.'` then `awk '$1 > 2.5'`) compares
  `"10.01" > "2.5"` as **strings**, because a `.`-bearing field is not numeric under a
  comma-radix locale, and silently returns false. **A gate built that way never fires, and
  this document would have claimed a quiet host it had never actually checked.** Replaced
  with `load1.py`, which reads `os.getloadavg()` and has no text step at all.

adjudication table and the scripts are in [`v0.14.0-raw/`](v0.14.0-raw/). The companion
allocation-only comparison is
[`v0130-vs-v0140-2026-09-06.md`](v0130-vs-v0140-2026-09-06.md); the prior release's
document is [`v0.13.0.md`](v0.13.0.md), which is **not** used as a baseline here — the
`v0.13.0` tree was checked out and **re-measured from scratch**.
