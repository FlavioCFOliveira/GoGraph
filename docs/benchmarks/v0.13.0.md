# Benchmarks — v0.13.0

Headline benchmark and load-test numbers for the GoGraph **v0.13.0** release, measured
first-hand as a **direct, interleaved A/B against the `v0.12.0` tree on this host**.

> **What moved in v0.13.0.** One thing enormously, one thing badly, and most things not
> at all. Sprints 352 and 353 landed **27 `perf` commits**, each measured in isolation
> against its own immediate predecessor; this document is the first measurement of what
> the release delivers **as a whole**.
>
> **What a consumer actually feels: the Cypher read path.** A read transaction under
> concurrent load is **−73.04 % geomean** across the published ladder
> (`ReadTx_LockFree` **−87.85 % at 1** to **−91.40 % at 1024**; `ReadTx_WriterLock`
> −23.28 % to **−52.56 %**), and it is **not only a CPU win**: `ReadTx_LockFree` drops from
> **249 to 37 allocations** and **82.0 → 4.1 KiB** per operation (**−85.1 %** and
> **−95.0 %**), all p=0.002 (§3.7.3). On the operator side, `cypher/exec` is **−18.93 %
> geomean** with `Scan_PerNode` **−69.97 %** and `Sort_10k` **−43.59 %** (§4.1), and
> labelled `count(*)` is **−87.65 % geomean**, its two serial spellings collapsing
> **2.61 ms → 880 ns** and **5.42 ms → 881 ns** with allocations **199 796 → 19** (§4.3).
>
> **The largest single number** is still the striped metrics counter:
> `BenchmarkIncCounterParallel` goes **47.74 → 1.881 ns/op at 8 goroutines**
> (**−96.06 %**, **25.4×**) and **49.70 → 1.754 ns/op at 1024** (**−96.47 %**, **28.3×**),
> turning a structure that *anti-scaled* — per-op cost rising **5.8×** as goroutines were
> added — into one that scales, per-op cost **falling 5.3×** (§3.1). Also
> `graph/index/hash` `SeekSingleton/Append` **1.40× at every level**, zero-allocation on
> both arms (§3.3).
>
> **The loss.** `graph/index/btree` `BenchmarkIndex_LookupHot` — the *only* B-tree
> benchmark that exists in both trees — is **23.1 % to 30.5 % slower** (geomean
> **+26.93 %**, p=0.002), with `B/op` and `allocs/op` byte-identical. The commit that
> plausibly caused it reported its own uncontended cost as **6.66 %** (§6.1).
>
> **The caveat that outranks both.** Sprint 353's three headline contention claims —
> `34.6×` btree at 1024, `1.91×` hash at 1024, `4.813×` hash at 8 — **cannot be verified
> at release level at all**, because the benchmarks that demonstrate them are **new in
> `v0.13.0`** and have no `v0.12.0` counterpart. They rest solely on each commit's own
> local A/B (§5.1). And of the benchmarks the ladder originally named, **four turned out
> not to measure concurrency at all** (§3.0).

> Re-measure on your own hardware before relying on any absolute number; these are
> medians of interleaved A/B runs from a documented session, not a guarantee.

## The confound that is absent this time, stated first

`go.mod` at `v0.12.0` and at `release/0.13.0` is **byte-identical** — verified by `diff`,
same `toolchain` directive, same direct and indirect dependency set. Both arms were
compiled by the **same local toolchain, go1.27.1**, from their own trees, and run
**interleaved on the same host in the same session**.

This is the first release since `v0.11.0` whose cross-release deltas are **not** mixed
with a compiler change. Every figure below is therefore attributable to GoGraph's own
source difference in a way `v0.12.0`'s cross-release column was not.

**The published `v0.12.0` figures are not the base arm here.** They were taken on
**go1.27.0**; the local toolchain is **go1.27.1**. `docs/benchmarks/v0.12.0.md` is not
cited as a baseline anywhere in this document — the `v0.12.0` tree was checked out into a
git worktree and **re-measured from scratch** for exactly this reason.

### Method

Every figure comes from `scratchpad/ab_bench.sh`, which:

- **interleaves the arms one repetition at a time**, and **alternates which arm runs
  first** on odd/even rounds, so neither arm systematically inherits the other's cache and
  thermal aftermath;
- **gates each block on a 1-minute load average below 2.5** before it opens, and records
  the figure every round started at;
- **fails loudly when a regex matches in only one arm**, so a benchmark that exists in one
  tree and not the other can never be reported as "no change".

Each arm runs its **own** pre-compiled test binary, so `-test.bench='^Benchmark'` selects
that tree's own benchmark set.

### The harness was repaired mid-campaign, and why that matters to a reader

The four `r-*` blocks were measured **after** both defects in §7 were fixed: benchmark
`stderr` is now diverted to `ab/<label>.stderr.log` instead of being merged into the data
stream, and only lines matching a strict result-line regex reach the data files.

**A reader should know that "clean" was not free.** Before the repair, a block could report
a full result-line count and still be losing lines silently. The repaired run of the
package that previously lost **60 of 60 lines** diverted **487 `WARN` lines** to stderr and
leaked **zero** into the data; the `count` block diverted a further 48. All eight `r-*`
files were re-checked here independently: **0 malformed lines, 0 `WARN` leakage, matching
base/head counts.** The earlier blocks in §1–§3.6 were validated with the same regex after
the fact, which is how `ladder-txn` and `ladder-cypher-readtx` were caught at all.

**Total measurement window: 03:22–05:28 for §1–§3.7.2 (126 min), plus the four repaired
blocks.** Two runs remain cut (§5.4).

## 1. The noise floor — measured first, before any delta was attributed

**Nothing in this document was attributed before this block ran.** Both arms of §1 point
at the **same tree** (`v0.12.0`), compiled and run under the identical harness schedule,
so **every difference measured here is noise by construction**. This is the yardstick each
later delta must clear.

Raw: [`v0.13.0-raw/noise-search.*`](v0.13.0-raw/), [`noise-lpg.*`](v0.13.0-raw/),
[`noise-cypher.*`](v0.13.0-raw/). `-count=8` per arm.

| Metric | Comparisons | Significant at p<0.05 | Largest \|median drift\| | Geomean drift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **sec/op** | 14 | **0** (min p = 0.130) | **0.87 %** | −0.09 %, −0.05 %, −0.49 % |
| **B/op** | 14 | **0** | **0.00 %** | +0.00 % |
| **allocs/op** | 14 | **0** | **0.00 %** | +0.00 % |

The largest same-vs-same drift was `LabelRead/deltas=on/no-live-delta`, 10.30 ns → 10.21 ns
(−0.87 %, p=0.223). Within-arm spread ran **±1–2 %** on `sec/op`. Every `B/op` and
`allocs/op` comparison was non-significant with a geomean drift of ±0.00 %, and every
sample was equal in **12 of 14** `B/op` comparisons and **13 of 14** `allocs/op`
comparisons.

**The floor this establishes:**

- **`sec/op` — ±0.9 %.** A delta smaller than this is reported below as *within noise*,
  never as a change, regardless of its p-value.
- **`B/op` and `allocs/op` — 0.0 %.** Allocation figures are exactly reproducible on this
  host under this harness, so **any** non-zero allocation delta below is real.

**This floor is much tighter than the 12.77 % this project recorded a release ago, and the
difference is methodological, not luck.** The 12.77 % figure came from two runs in
**different sessions** on **separately built trees**. Everything here is a single session,
interleaved arm-by-arm, on binaries built back-to-back. **The ±0.9 % floor licenses only
comparisons made this way.** It does **not** license comparing any number below against a
figure from another session — including the published `v0.12.0` document, where the
historical 12.77 % cross-session floor still applies.

## 3. The concurrency ladder — 1 / 8 / 64 / 256 / 1024

CLAUDE.md's EXTREME/MASSIVE Concurrent Ready mandate publishes this ladder, so it is swept
with `-test.cpu=1,8,64,256,1024` on benchmarks common to both trees. `-count=6` per arm.

**Scaling is reported as a ratio to each arm's own level-1 cell**, never as a raw
cross-level number — a commit in this very release
(`fix(bench/contention): normalise every ceiling by its own level-1 cell`) exists because
the raw form claimed one direction for both arms.

**Read `ns/op` correctly under `b.RunParallel`:** `b.N` is the total iteration count across
all goroutines, so `ns/op` is **inverse aggregate throughput**, not per-goroutine latency.

### 3.0 Which of these is actually a ladder — read this before any figure below

`-test.cpu=1,8,64,256,1024` only builds a goroutine ladder for a benchmark that calls
**`b.RunParallel`**. On a serial benchmark it varies **`GOMAXPROCS` and nothing else**: it
creates no concurrent reader, no contention, and no scaling curve — what it moves is
mostly GC parallelism.

Every benchmark in this section was classified by parsing its function body — for a
`RunParallel` call **and** for a hand-rolled `go func` / `sync.WaitGroup` fan-out. Both
checks are necessary, and each one alone gets a different benchmark wrong:

- **A name is not evidence.** `BenchmarkOutDegreeByType_Parallel` is **serial** despite
  its name.
- **Absence of `RunParallel` is not evidence of serial, either.**
  `BenchmarkCommitConcurrent` spawns `goroutines` real goroutines behind a `WaitGroup` and
  drives genuinely concurrent commits — it simply does not use `b.RunParallel`. Screening
  on `RunParallel` alone would have misfiled it as serial.

| Benchmark | Package | Concurrent? | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| `IncCounterParallel` | `internal/metrics/prometheus` | **yes** | `b.RunParallel` |
| `NodeMetadataReadParallel` | `graph/lpg` | **yes** | `b.RunParallel` |
| `Barrier_BareRWMutexParallel` | `graph/lpg` | **yes** | `b.RunParallel` |
| `BoltRecordEncodeParallel` | `bolt/server` | **yes** | `b.RunParallel` |
| `CommitConcurrent` | `store/txn` | **yes** | hand-rolled `go func` + `WaitGroup`, 1/8/64/**256** |
| `WriteScaling_{StoreAPI,Cypher}` | `store/txn` | **yes** | own writer ladder in sub-benchmarks |
| `Index_LookupHot` | `graph/index/btree` | no — **serial** | — |
| `Index_LookupHot` | `graph/index/hash` | no — **serial** | — |
| `Index_SeekSingleton` | `graph/index/hash` | no — **serial** | — |
| `OutDegreeByType_Parallel` | `graph/lpg` | no — **serial, despite the name** | — |

So §3.2, §3.3 and the `OutDegreeByType_Parallel` rows of §3.5 are **GOMAXPROCS-sensitivity
measurements of a serial path**, and are reported as such. They are not scaling results and
must not be read as any.

§3.7 is the **corrected ladder**: five further benchmarks, common to both trees, that
genuinely call `RunParallel`.

### 3.1 `internal/metrics/prometheus` — the release's headline

`BenchmarkIncCounterParallel`, a genuine `b.RunParallel` benchmark on the module's most
contended metrics path. Raw: [`v0.13.0-raw/ladder-prom.*`](v0.13.0-raw/).

| Goroutines | base ns/op | head ns/op | Δ | speed-up | p |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8.626 ± 2 % | 9.343 ± 2 % | **+8.32 %** | 0.92× | 0.002 |
| 8 | 47.740 ± 10 % | **1.881 ± 12 %** | **−96.06 %** | **25.4×** | 0.002 |
| 64 | 50.195 ± 3 % | **1.779 ± 5 %** | **−96.45 %** | **28.2×** | 0.002 |
| 256 | 50.315 ± 3 % | **1.831 ± 5 %** | **−96.36 %** | **27.5×** | 0.002 |
| 1024 | 49.700 ± 2 % | **1.754 ± 4 %** | **−96.47 %** | **28.3×** | 0.002 |

geomean **−92.79 %**. `B/op` and `allocs/op` are **0 on both arms at every level**, all
samples equal — the win costs no allocation.

**Normalised to each arm's own level-1 cell, which is the claim that actually matters:**

| Goroutines | base, vs its own level 1 | head, vs its own level 1 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | **5.53× worse** | **4.97× better** |
| 64 | **5.82× worse** | **5.25× better** |
| 256 | **5.83× worse** | **5.10× better** |
| 1024 | **5.76× worse** | **5.33× better** |

**This is a change of sign, not a change of degree.** At `v0.12.0` the counter
*anti-scaled*: adding goroutines made aggregate throughput **fall to ~0.17×** of the
single-goroutine rate, which is the signature of a single contended cache line. At
`v0.13.0` throughput **rises 5.0×–5.3×** with concurrency and stays flat from 8 to 1024 — a
**128× over-subscription** of 10 physical cores absorbed without a cliff. Against CLAUDE.md's
"throughput must rise with the cores made available to it", `v0.12.0` failed this probe and
`v0.13.0` passes it.

**The price is real and is not rounded away: +8.32 % (p=0.002) at one goroutine**, or
+0.72 ns. That is above the ±0.9 % floor and is reported as a loss in §6. It is *consistent
with* the per-core striping the commit describes — an extra indirection on the uncontended
path — but **that attribution is not established by this measurement**: a profile of one arm
cannot explain a difference between two arms, and no third A/B isolating the indirection was
run.

### 3.4 `bolt/server` — no measurable change

`BenchmarkBoltRecordEncodeParallel`. Raw: [`v0.13.0-raw/ladder-bolt.*`](v0.13.0-raw/).

**All ten cells are within noise** (p=0.093–0.937; geomean +0.67 %), with `B/op` and
`allocs/op` identical (48 B / 4 allocs on `baseline`, **zero** on `fast`). Both arms scale
the same way: `baseline` 81.0 → 16.7 ns from 1 to 8 goroutines, `fast` 29.4 → 5.4 ns.

**The release's Bolt claim is not visible here, and this is not evidence against it.**
`perf(bolt/server): take the transaction registry off the per-message path — 9.40x worse
becomes 6.10x better` targets the per-message transaction-registry path; record *encoding*
is a different path, and it is the only Bolt parallel benchmark common to both trees.
**The Bolt claim is unmeasured at release level**, not refuted — see §5.

recorded here so a future cycle can look again with more samples, and it is **not** counted

## 5. What this snapshot does NOT measure

This section is longer than usual because the release's own headline claims fall into it.

## 6. What got slower — measured, not minimised

Five losses clear the ±0.9 % floor of §1. All are `-race` **off**.

### 6.1 `graph/index/btree` `Index_LookupHot` — **+23.1 % to +30.5 %**, the largest loss

The single most serious number in this release. **Every one of the five levels is slower**,
p=0.002 at each, geomean **+26.93 %** — with `B/op` (248) and `allocs/op` (10)
**byte-identical on both arms, all samples equal**. Identical allocation with a 27 % time
increase means more work per lookup, not more garbage.

The size relative to the floor makes this unambiguous: 26.9 % against a 0.9 % floor, on the
same host, in the same session that measured the `search` control at ±0.7 %.

#### The commit predicted 6.66 %. The release delivers 26.93 %.

`fcce83ac` (`perf(index/btree): immutable spine plus per-entry locks, 34.6x at 1024`) is
candidly explicit about the cost it accepted, and its own message reads:

> Level 1 regresses 6.66% (40.4ns -> 43.2ns/op), roughly 3x its own noise floor and
> therefore real. It is reported, not tuned away: one uncontended RLock became an atomic
> pointer load, a pointer hop and an uncontended Mutex.

| Source | Fixture | Uncontended cost |
|---|---|---:|
| `fcce83ac`'s own sweep | 40.4 → 43.2 ns/op | **+6.66 %** |
| This release-level A/B | `Index_LookupHot`, 316.4 → 412.8 ns/op | **+30.47 %** (geomean **+26.93 %**) |

**State the gap accurately: these are two different fixtures, not one measurement
contradicting itself.** A 40 ns operation and a 316 ns operation that allocates 248 B in
10 allocations are not the same shape, so this is not evidence that `fcce83ac`'s figure was
wrong on its own benchmark. What it *is* evidence of is that **the uncontended cost of the
change is fixture-dependent, and on an ordinary lookup shape it is roughly four times the
figure the commit's own probe reported.** A reader who took 6.66 % as the price of this
change would be understating it by ~20 percentage points on this path.

**Cause: unestablished.** `fcce83ac` and `6dface7d` (`perf(index/btree): slab-allocate
entries`) are **candidates** — both restructure this lookup path — but the release delta
spans both, and attributing it requires a third A/B that this campaign did not run.
**Recommended next step:** interleaved A/Bs of `fcce83ac^`→`fcce83ac` and
`6dface7d^`→`6dface7d` on `BenchmarkIndex_LookupHot` alone at `-count=10`, to apportion the
26.93 % between them. Validation would be `go test -race ./graph/index/...` plus the
openCypher TCK gate.

**And the trade cannot be judged from this document.** The `34.6x at 1024` that was bought
with this cost is measured on `bench/contention`, which **does not exist in `v0.12.0`** and
therefore could not be verified here (§5.1). A 34.6× contended win may well justify a 27 %
serial loss on a module whose first mandate is extreme concurrency — but **this campaign
measured only one side of that trade**, and no conclusion about its net value is claimed.

8.626 → 9.343 ns/op, p=0.002, zero allocations on both arms. This is the uncontended price
of the change that delivers −96 % at 8–1024 goroutines (§3.1). Against that trade the loss

### 6.3 `graph/index/hash` `SeekSingleton/Bitmap` at 1 goroutine — **+7.06 %**

220.8 → 236.5 ns/op, p=0.002, `B/op` 408 and `allocs/op` 11 identical. The same shape as

### 6.4 `search` `Yen_K100` — **+2.42 %**

14.50 → 14.85 ms, p=0.002, in a package that changed by **78 lines** and whose other three

| 64 | 1.683 KiB | 1.725 KiB | **+2.50 %** | 0.037 |
| 256 | 3.005 KiB | 3.000 KiB | −0.17 % | 0.699 |
| 1024 | 7.824 KiB | 8.036 KiB | +2.71 % | 0.589 |

`allocs/op` is **identical** at every level (22, 21, 20, 19, 21) — the same number of
allocations, each slightly larger — and time and throughput are unchanged (§3.6). Against
the 0.0 % `B/op` floor of §1 the first three rows are real. Roughly 30 bytes per durable
commit; small, but CLAUDE.md's ULTRA EFFICIENT mandate treats an avoidable byte as a defect,
so it is filed rather than rounded away.

### 6.6 `graph/mvcc` `PublishConcurrent/legacy` at 1024 — **+2.08 %**

94.94 → 96.91 ns/op, p=0.015, the **only** one of `ladder-mvcc`'s twenty cells to clear the
floor (§3.7.2). It is on the *legacy* publish arm, not the frontier arm, and the same
sub-benchmark is within noise at 1, 8, 64 and 256. Small, isolated, and reported because
2.08 % is above the 0.9 % floor — not because it is believed to matter.

### 6.7 The four regressions on the changed Cypher surfaces

All measured on the repaired harness at `-count=6`, race off.

| Benchmark | Block | base → head | Δ | p |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `Drain_Throughput` | `cypher/exec` | 14.24 → 16.07 µs | **+12.87 %** | 0.002 |
| `AllNodesScan_PerNodeAllocCost` | `cypher/exec` | 10.29 → 10.65 µs | **+3.45 %** | 0.002 |
| `CountAllNodes` | `cypher` | 17.19 → 17.72 µs | **+3.09 %** | 0.002 |
| `ColumnarAgg_MinMaxGroupBy/row_off` | `cypher/exec` | 53.34 → 54.61 ms | **+2.38 %** | 0.009 |

**`Drain_Throughput` at +12.87 % is the largest of the four** and sits in a block whose
geomean is **−18.93 %** — so the operator layer got substantially faster overall while its
drain path got measurably slower. `allocs/op` is identical (1 013, all samples equal), so
this is CPU-side.

**`CountAllNodes` carries an allocation component**, and it is the only regression in this
release that does: `allocs/op` **25 → 27 (+8.00 %, p=0.002)**. Against §1's 0.0 %
allocation floor two extra allocations per operation is unambiguous. It sits in a block
whose allocation geomean is **−90.12 %**.

**`AllNodesScan_PerNodeAllocCost` is worth a second look by name alone** — a benchmark that
exists to track per-node allocation cost regressed 3.45 % in time while its allocation
count did not move. Cause unestablished.

None of the four is attributed to a commit; no third A/B was run (§5.9).

### Reported as inconclusive rather than as a regression

`graph/lpg` `OutDegreeByType_Parallel` moved **+0.37 % to +0.70 %** with p<0.05 in 6 of 15
cells and a consistent sign — **below the floor**, so not counted (§3.5).

## 7. Two instrument defects found during this campaign

Both are recorded because each silently produced a wrong or absent result, and neither is
fixed in the harness as shipped.

- **An empty bash array under `set -u` aborts every benchmark invocation on bash 3.2.**
  `"${extra[@]}"` with no extra flags raises `unbound variable` on macOS's system bash
  (3.2.57), so the first Block-0 attempt ran eight full rounds and produced **zero result
  lines**. It was caught only by the harness's own empty-arm guard. The harness's self-test
  had never exercised this path because it always passed `-test.benchtime`. Fixed in-session
  with `${extra[@]+"${extra[@]}"}`; the original is retained as `ab_bench.sh.orig`.
- **A log line written onto a benchmark result line destroys it silently, and it cost this
  campaign a whole run.** The `WARN cypher: engine constructed over a non-multigraph
  graph…` warning is emitted while a benchmark result line is being written, splicing
  itself into the line. It hit **`ladder-txn`** (30 of 84 lines per arm, §3.6) and
  **`ladder-cypher-readtx`**, where it destroyed **60 of 60 lines in both arms** — a total
  loss of a 457 s run. A `benchstat` that simply sees fewer samples reads exactly like "no
  regression", so this defect fails silently in the dangerous direction.

  Every raw file in this campaign was validated with a regex for well-formed result lines
  (`^Benchmark\S*\s+\d+\s+[\d.]+\s+\S+`). Of the 13 labels run on the original harness,
  **11 were clean, one was partially corrupted and repaired, and one was a total loss** — a
  defect rate that would have been invisible without the check.

  **Both defects were fixed inside this campaign and the fix was verified, not assumed.**
  Benchmark `stderr` is now diverted to `ab/<label>.stderr.log` rather than merged with
  `2>&1`, and only strict result lines reach the data files. Re-run on the exact package
  that had lost 60 of 60 lines, **487 `WARN` lines were diverted and zero leaked**; the
  `count` block diverted 48 more. The four `r-*` blocks were re-checked here independently:
  **0 malformed, 0 leaked, matching base/head counts** (§4, §3.7.3). The underlying warning
  should still not be emitted from a benchmark at all.
