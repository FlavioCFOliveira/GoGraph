## Overview

**100 commits** — 30 fixes, 27 performance changes, 16 documentation, 12 test, 4 features,
4 build changes, 3 chores, and 4 merges. Counted at `b523878b`, the last commit before release
preparation; the release-prep commits that carry this entry are necessarily not in their own
count. **Two sprints delivered work in this window — 352,
*GoGraph module deep profiling and optimization* (31 tasks), and 353, *GoGraph Optimization
Laboratory* (55 tasks) — and all 86 closed.** 731 files changed, 74 802 insertions,
6 671 deletions (`git diff --shortstat v0.12.0..b523878b`).

This is the sixteenth published release of **GoGraph**, a Go module for graph persistence,
manipulation and fast search. It is a pre-1.0 **MINOR** release, and it is a different kind of
release from `v0.12.0` in both directions. Where `v0.12.0` was a testing-and-correctness release
that added no engine capability and broke nothing, `v0.13.0` is a **performance and
correctness-hardening** release that changes how several hot paths are built and ships **seven
breaking changes**.

Three things define it.

**It is the module's two deep-profiling and optimisation cycles.** Sprint 352 audited the module
for bottlenecks and then worked the Cypher read path; sprint 353 built a committed contention
observatory and worked the index, `graph/lpg`, `metrics` and Bolt surfaces under concurrency.
**27 of the 100 commits are `perf`**, against 4 in the whole of `v0.12.0`. The largest single
result is that four workloads which **anti-scaled** — throughput falling as goroutines rise while
cores sit idle, which is contention and not oversubscription — no longer do.

**Seven changes are marked breaking, and every one of them makes a previously *silent* failure
loud.** Two durable formats now refuse a field they would have truncated or written unreadably;
three `graph/lpg` removal primitives now report a refusal their callers were journalling an undo
for regardless; a panic in the commit window no longer wedges every later committer on the store;
and Cypher accepts `EXPLAIN` and `PROFILE` as statement prefixes. All seven are set out under
[Breaking changes](#breaking-changes--and-what-to-do-about-them), with the caller-visible
consequence of each.

**A great many published claims were refuted by their own measurement, and are recorded as
refuted rather than quietly dropped.** This is worth stating up front, because it is most of what
these two sprints actually produced. The write barrier that the round-2 inventory ranked at
72–99 % of write-path blocked time holds **zero** of it. The Bolt transport the scaling column was
suspected of throttling is not the limiter, and the published ratios *understate* a real socket
rather than flattering it. A ceiling table published as "every arm is a lower bound" was
**filtered**, and three of the seven omitted arms sit above 1.00. The full list is in
[Premises refuted](#premises-refuted--the-negative-results).

**Nothing in the supply chain moved.** `go.mod` and `go.sum` are byte-identical to `v0.12.0`, so a
cross-release performance comparison against `v0.12.0` mixes **no** compiler or dependency change
with GoGraph's own — which is exactly what makes such a comparison meaningful this time and did
not hold last time.

## Semantic-versioning rationale

`v0.13.0` is a pre-1.0 **MINOR** release. Three facts decide it, and all three were established by
audit of the tree rather than asserted:

1. **Seven commits carry a `!` marker**, and two further exported signatures changed without one.
   Under [docs/semver.md](../docs/semver.md) a removed exported identifier or an incompatibly
   changed signature is a MAJOR-class change; **pre-1.0 the minor digit absorbs it**, exactly as it
   did for `v0.11.0`'s 31 breaking changes and for `v0.9.0`'s removal of `ParallelScan`.
2. **19 exported identifiers were removed**, all in `cypher/exec` and all previously unwired: the
   `ChunkPool` family and the `RowSlab`/`SlabPool` arena. `docs/semver.md` **does** list
   `cypher/exec` in the public API surface, so this is stated as an API removal rather than
   described as a cleanup.
3. **77 exported identifiers were added**, of which 8 are in `cypher/parser/gen`, which
   `docs/semver.md` excludes from the public API by intent. The largest groups are `cypher/exec`
   (30), `cypher/expr` (13), `cypher/parser` (6), `cypher/ir` (5) and `graph/lpg` (5).
   No exported struct field was removed.

**On-disk formats did not move.** The snapshot manifest stays at version 3 and `csrfile` at version
1. What changed is that both durable writers now **refuse** a field their own readers are required
to reject, instead of writing one — see [Breaking changes](#breaking-changes--and-what-to-do-about-them).

**Why this is not `v1.0.0`.** The gate status is unchanged: **three of the four documented `1.0.0`
gates are met** — execution-level TCK is at 100 % against a ≥ 95 % requirement, every `T-`
divergence is resolved, and the local gate is green. **Gate 4 is not met**, for a sixth consecutive
cycle: it requires the soak report in `soak-artefacts/` to reflect a run against the release commit,
and those artefacts are from **2026-05-30 at commit `b5453b9`**. That gate, together with
[what this release does not establish](#what-this-release-does-not-establish), is what stands
between GoGraph and `1.0.0`.

## Highlights

### Contention stopped being inferred and started being measured

Before this release, **nothing in the repository enabled Go's contention profilers.** No source
called `runtime.SetMutexProfileFraction` or `SetBlockProfileRate`; the only traces were a comment in
`bench/audit352` and prose in `docs/` describing ad-hoc `go test -mutexprofile` runs that were never
committed. Every prior contention claim this project made therefore rested on the shape of a
throughput curve, never on lock-site attribution.

`bench/contention` is the instrument that closes the gap, and two of its properties are load-bearing
— each established by a **control that failed first**:

- **One fresh child process per window.** Mutex and block profiles are cumulative for a process's
  lifetime with no reset API, so an in-process control reported 101 ms against a fixture it never
  invoked. The heap is cumulative the same way: when both windows shared a child, the profiled
  window inherited a warm heap and measured *faster* while carrying a full-rate profiler, in five
  runs of six.
- **Ranking by `-cum -lines`, not flat.** Flat time on a mutex profile belongs to the release site
  in the standard library, so the ranking was `sync.(*Mutex).Unlock` and `runtime.unlock` with no
  `file:line` anywhere — the instrument's whole purpose, undelivered.

The first full sweep found four workloads that **anti-scale**: `index-count-hot` at 0.391× at 8
goroutines, `index-btree-rw` at 0.445×, `index-count-spread` at 0.917×, and `cypher-mixed-rw`
collapsing to 0.096× at 1024. Every one of those is closed below. Three inherited leads were
re-validated at HEAD and **all three failed**: the documented 0.828× write-scaling collapse was dead
(1.942× at 8), the vacuum was 2.42 % rather than the 30.64 % top site, and `NodeLabels` heap
contention was 0.63 ms of 58.31 ms rather than "100 %".

### The four anti-scaling curves are gone

| Workload | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| `index-btree-rw` @1024 | 3 788 293 ops/s, scaling 0.153× | 131 225 618 ops/s, scaling 5.675× | immutable spine + per-entry locks (#2683) |
| `cypher-mixed-rw` @1024 | 9 359 ops/s, scaling 0.101× | 384 960 ops/s, scaling 0.829× | per-label churn gate on the suspect walk (#2686) |
| `index-count-spread` @1024 | 54.5 M ops/s, scaling 0.538× | 309.3 M ops/s, scaling 2.508× | shard key + atomic cell (#2682) |
| `index-count-hot` @1024 | 24.1 M ops/s, scaling 0.147× | 80.6 M ops/s, **flat** | same (#2682), then striped shard lock (#2696) |

`index-count-hot`'s ratio cannot exceed 1.000× **by construction** and that is stated rather than
worked around: every writer increments one counter on one cache line, so the bound is coherence
traffic, not locking — a ceiling probe measured 0.725× even with a perfect lock-free write path.
What changed is that its curve is now flat across the ladder instead of collapsing.

`cypher-mixed-rw`'s ratios **understate** it, and are published with the absolutes beside them: its
level-1 throughput itself rose 4.99×, because a single reader now skips the suspect walk entirely,
so every rung improved while the ratio fell only because the denominator improved most.

### A profile alone would have sent three of these at the wrong target

The most useful methodological result of the two sprints is how often the profile's top line was not
the lever.

- **`cypher-mixed-rw`** (#2686). The task blamed `correctBitmapOver`, which is 54.3 % of CPU at
  1024. Two ceiling probes overturned it: deleting the correction alone bought 3.84×, deleting the
  **gathering** and the correction bought 29.93×. The 7.81× between them is the gathering —
  `suspectNodes` is only 6.6–9.6 % of CPU but **28.12 % of block time**, because it `RLock`s 64
  label plus 64 life shards **twice per read**, making every reader a 128-shard barrier against every
  writer. And the correction it was named after does no work: counters over ~120 000 corrections
  measured **0.0000 bits changed per read** while walking 131 to 1 049 suspects each time. The zero
  is trustworthy because the positive control fires — when writers touch the scanned label the same
  counter reads 0.708 and 6.61 bits/read.
- **`index-count`** (#2696). The inventory attributed the collapse to `atomic.Int32.Add` at 44.85 %
  of CPU, and the task derived from it that the counter was the contended object. It was not, and
  the **symbol type was the tell**: `pprof -peek` shows 100 % of that `Int32.Add` is called from
  `RWMutex.RLock`/`RUnlock`, while the count cells it guards are `atomic.Int64` at 12.60 %. The
  contended word was the shard lock's reader counter. A single-variable bake-off settled the design:
  striping the counter buys 1.227×, removing the lock buys 2.304×.
- **`index-hash`** (#2699). The same mistake, one index over. The task blamed a shared counter on the
  read path, citing `atomic.(*Int32).Add` at 24.13 % beside `Cardinality`. The **code** said
  otherwise before any profile did: `hashShard.nonEmpty` is `atomic.Int64` and `Cardinality` never
  touches it, while `sync.RWMutex.RLock` is `atomic.AddInt32` on its `readerCount`. An `Int64`
  counter cannot emit an `Int32.Add` sample.

### Two silent wrong answers, each uncovered by removing the cost that hid it

**`PROFILE` rendered a different plan from the one that ran, and reported `rows=0`** (#2665).
`MATCH (a:Person)-[:KNOWS]->(b:Person) RETURN b.salary` returned 960 000 rows from `Run`, `EXPLAIN`
rendered `ColumnarProject`/`ColumnarFilter`/`columnarExpand`, and `PROFILE` rendered
`Filter`/`Expand` — a different plan — with `rows=0` at every level above the leaf and **no error**.
Three defects chained, and only the chain produces the wrong answer: a recogniser asserting
`expandOp.(*exec.Expand)` declined because `Wrap` preserves every *interface* but cannot preserve a
*concrete* type; the decline happened **after** `buildOperator` had already written the caller's
schema, so the fall-through rebuilt the same subtree on a polluted map; and `Expand`'s "row too
narrow, skip silently" guard converted that corruption into a wrong answer. A second instance of the
same class had already been **documented as intended** in a code comment.

**Reverse and undirected `Expand` under a correlated `Apply` returned rows for nodes with no
matching edge** (#2251). `MATCH (a:P) WHERE EXISTS { MATCH (a)<-[r]-(x) RETURN x } RETURN a.id`
returned `[b c]` where `[b]` is correct — `c` has no incoming relationship at all. `Expand.Init`
reset `srcID` and `fwdDone` but not the reverse cursor or the `CREATE`-multiplicity pending queue,
and `Init` runs once per **outer** row under `Apply`, so a half-consumed run is the normal case
rather than an edge case. The lesson is the class, not the instance: **the typed form of that query
was accidentally correct**, because the per-slot type test was recovering each reverse slot's forward
position from a not-yet-loaded sentinel and could never find a counterpart — so an accidental cost
was acting as an unintended validity filter on the operator's own stale state. Removing the cost
uncovered the defect the cost was hiding.

### Three ACID defects in the life-record store, diagnosed before any of them was worked

A 1000-seed sweep of the DST's MVCC-sessions mode surfaced three violation families the gates had
never reached. All three are **engine** defects under Compliance Mandate 2, and the diagnosis
(#2723,
[`docs/mvcc-life-record-defects-2026-09-03.md`](../docs/mvcc-life-record-defects-2026-09-03.md))
deliberately implemented **no fix**: each family needed its own task and its own regression gate, and
two of them sit on a seam that had already produced #2443, #2444, #2445, #2686 and #2687 with each
fix opening a mirror of the last.

The verdicts rest on one-factor reproductions against the real store-backed engine rather than on the
oracle's classification, because the immediately preceding task presented an identical symptom —
"engine committed but oracle refused" — and turned out to be a **harness** bug (#2717). Assuming
these were engine defects because they are labelled ACID would have been the same error with the
opposite sign.

All three were then fixed, and **with the third commit both DST arms are clean**. Rates over 1000
seeds per arm at `Ticks=1200`: the no-crash arm goes 4 unclean → 2 → 0, and the crash arm 5 → 1 → 0.

The diagnosis also found a **DST fidelity defect** on the way, and it constrains every regression
test written against that mode: seed 790 is **not deterministic** — 12 processes gave the same
finding at three different ticks. `RunMVCCSessions` documents the whole run as a pure function of
the seed, but `mvcc_vacuum.go` runs `vacuumLoop` on a background goroutine with wall-clock backoff,
and a leaked bare node is invisible until its life record is reclaimed. The defect is stable; only
the detection tick moves. **Any seed regression test on a reclamation-sensitive mode must therefore
not assert an exact tick**, and the committed gates do not.

### The vulnerability gate that never existed

The task was opened because `govulncheck` was reported broken. Investigating it found something
larger: **there was no gate.** `govulncheck` appeared in no Makefile target, no `make ci` path, and
no `.sh`/`.yml`/`.yaml` file anywhere in the repository — confirmed by `command grep -rn` and an
independent Python filesystem walk, 7 hits all prose. The vulnerability check was never automated; it
was a command someone ran by hand, which is exactly why it could stop working unnoticed. **A gate
nobody invokes cannot fail loudly — it simply never runs.**

That reframes the `v0.12.0` incident this task was opened for. The "CVE gate" that "silently exited 0
with no analysis" was a manual step, so nothing was watching it.

`make vulncheck` now exists, is wired into `ci`, `ci-soak` and `ci-nightly` immediately after
`build`, and `release-preflight` inherits it. **The assertion is the point, and it is not the exit
status.** See [Security](#security) for how it is constructed and how it was proven able to fail.

## The two sprints, in sequence

### Sprint 352 — GoGraph module deep profiling and optimization (31 tasks, 32 commits)

The sprint opened with an exhaustive efficiency and performance audit measured fresh at HEAD
([`docs/audit-bottlenecks-2026-08-27.md`](../docs/audit-bottlenecks-2026-08-27.md), #2643): eleven
ranked bottlenecks and seven small-change/big-gain items, each with the profile it came from and the
workload that exposed it, and **ten premises recorded as refuted**, including two of the audit's own
hypotheses. No figure was carried forward from the 2026-08-11 peer audit or from any backlog task
description; those served only as a checklist of places to point an instrument. Two caveats bound
every scaling number in it and are stated rather than implied: this M4 is **4 performance + 6
efficiency cores**, so a ~3× ceiling is hardware rather than a defect, and the peer CPU model cannot
run on Darwin at all, so the report contains no GoGraph-versus-peer figure.

What followed worked the Cypher read path, top-down by measured share: the per-outer-row adjacency
rebuild under `Apply` (#2646), the labelled count pushdown's parallelism gate (#2654), `ORDER BY` key
materialisation (#2652), the result-budget counters (#2649), the row-context schema walk (#2645), the
evaluation-state sentinel threaded through the row map (#2653), the per-relationship property map
(#2388), projection-item fusion (#2658), the relationship-type column (#2251), the aggregate's lost
columnar chain (#2655), `ORDER BY SKIP LIMIT` fusion (#2509), block-form `COUNT` (#2648) and
`count(var)` normalisation (#2657).

Along the way the sprint had to repair its own instruments. `make ci` **could not fail on a test
failure** (#2672): `.SHELLFLAGS` requires GNU Make 3.82 and macOS ships 3.81, so `-e -u -o pipefail`
never applied and a run containing `FAIL bench/audit352` read `MAKE_CI_EXIT=0` and continued through
lint and cover-gate. Three `bench/audit352` instruments asserted **absolutes** about quantities they
did not own — the process's malloc counter, the process's allocation stream, the machine's cores —
and were rebuilt to assert **differences** measured in the same window (#2666). The package had no
cost-budget entry and had never been measured in-suite at all (#2670, #2667).

#2672's own record notes that the next three gate runs immediately caught three real defects that
had been invisible. **Drafting these notes caught a fourth.** `make release-accuracy` — the target
the release CI job runs — could not execute at all on macOS: its README check echoed an unbraced
`$VERSION` immediately followed by an ellipsis character, and under bash 3.2 in a UTF-8 locale
`isalnum()` accepts the lead byte of a multi-byte character, so the shell read the variable name as
`VERSION` plus that byte, found it unset, and `set -u` aborted the target before it reached its later
checks. It had been latent for as long as the ellipsis has been there and inert for as long as
`-e -u -o pipefail` was. Reproduced in isolation with a braced control that passes, and fixed by
bracing the reference.

### Sprint 353 — GoGraph Optimization Laboratory (55 tasks, 63 commits)

The sprint built the contention observatory (#2678), extended it to the full module surface
(#2679, 19 workloads), and then re-baselined at HEAD after round 1's six fixes with a **round-2
inventory ranked by ceiling rather than by share** (#2690) — because share does not predict it:
`planCache.get` holds 61.48 % of its profile and its ceiling is 1.111×, while
`generation-publish-read` scales at 0.094× with 64 ms of mutex delay behind it, its cost being a
shared refcount atomic that **no blocked-time profiler can see**.

The fixes that followed are in [Performance](#performance--the-honest-account). What is worth
recording separately is how many of the sprint's own published numbers turned out to be wrong, and
that each correction is recorded **in place, visibly**, rather than by silently rewriting the section
it amends. Sites 1 and 3 of round 1 were mis-attributed (#2679, #2681, #2683). `index.Manager` was
said to have no engine caller on the strength of a grep **truncated at twenty lines** (#2690).
Ceiling arms were never normalised by their own level-1 cell, and the handicap table had been
**filtered** to the six arms below 1.00 before the conclusion "every ceiling is therefore a lower
bound" was drawn from it (#2712). A re-taken noise floor came back **byte-identical** to a run ten
minutes earlier, including its reported 102.86 s duration, in a window that finished in under a
second — Go's test cache had served the whole thing (#2713).

The sprint closed with a run of durability and MVCC work: the WAL length-prefix truncation (#2742),
the snapshot cap alignment (#2743), the apply-gate wedge (#2727), the two containment boundaries
(#2707), and the three life-record families (#2724, #2725, #2726). It also closed the lint
configuration properly (#2708, #2746) and created the vulnerability gate (#2722).

**What the sprint set out to find is not what it found.** Its own closing record names the recurring
result, eight times over: **the instruments lie.** Four godocs described behaviour the code does not
have, including one justifying a barrier by a reader type removed two sprints earlier; an observatory
republished a cached measurement as a fresh one; a vulnerability gate exited 0 without analysing
anything; two DST fixtures' oracles reported success while measuring nothing, one of them retiring
its entire role in silence — 192 contended transactions issued, **zero committed**, while the oracle
compared 0 against 0 and passed; a checkpoint gate judged a snapshot by matching **filenames** in a
manifest and then truncated the WAL behind one no reader could parse; and a lint suppression had been
hiding a live ACID breach. That last one is the sprint's most consequential result, and it is worth
being precise about how it was found: **removing a `G115` path exclusion that an earlier audit had
called "not a defect today" exposed five `uint16` WAL length prefixes with no bound**, while their
siblings were guarded. It was found because someone removed the exclusion and **looked**.

Scope grew twice on the user's decision — five tasks pulled in from the lint sweep, taking the sprint
from 50 to 55 — and **21 follow-up tasks were registered** rather than absorbed. The three largest
are in [Known issues](#known-issues).

## Performance — the honest account

### What this release delivers over `v0.12.0`, measured at both trees

Sprints 352 and 353 landed **27 `perf` commits**, each measured in isolation against its
immediate predecessor. A sum of 27 local A/Bs is **not** a release-level claim: local wins
compose, cancel, and occasionally regress a neighbour. So the two release trees were measured
against each other directly — a `git worktree` at the `v0.12.0` tag and this release candidate,
each compiled once, run **interleaved** one repetition at a time with the leading arm
alternating, `-race` off, `-count=6`, on a host gated below a 1-minute load average of 2.5.

**The noise floor was established before anything was attributed.** Both arms pointed at the
same tree, so every measured difference there is pure noise: **14 comparisons, 0 significant**,
largest median drift **0.87 % on sec/op**, **0.00 % on allocations**.

| Block | geomean | Largest single move |
|---|---:|---|
| Cypher count pushdown | **−87.65 %** | `Count_LabelStarBig_Serial` **−99.97 %**, ≈3 ms → 880 ns |
| Contended metric emission, 1→1024 | **−92.79 %** | `IncCounterParallel` **−96.47 %** at 1024 |
| Read transaction under concurrency, 1→1024 | **−73.04 %** | `ReadTx_LockFree` **−91.40 %** at 1024 |
| `cypher/exec` read-path operators | **−18.93 %** | `Scan_PerNode` −69.97 %; `Sort_10k` −43.59 % |
| Columnar query shapes | **−4.28 %** | five hop shapes −7.69 % to −9.42 % |
| `search` headline set (**control**) | +0.83 % | within noise, as a control must be |

The metric-emission result is the one that changes **character** rather than degree: `v0.12.0`
anti-scaled — cost *rising* 5.8× from 1 to 1024 goroutines — while `v0.13.0` scales, cost
*falling* 5.3×. That is the difference between a counter you must avoid on a hot path and one
you can leave in.

**Allocations moved as much as time did, on two of the four blocks — and the split is itself the
result.** The read-transaction ladder falls **−76.29 % allocs/op** and **−86.94 % B/op** in
geomean (`ReadTx_LockFree` 249 → **37**, `ReadTx_WriterLock` 306 → **114**), and the count block
**−90.12 % allocs/op** (`Count_LabelStarBig_Serial` **199 796 → 19**). What makes this structural
rather than a tuning win is that `v0.13.0`'s counts are **constant across the entire 1 → 1024
ladder** — 37 and 114 at every level — where `v0.12.0`'s drift upward with concurrency. The
`cypher/exec` operator block and the columnar shapes are by contrast **flat** (−0.12 % and
−0.19 %), so their time wins are CPU-side. The allocation work in this window targeted the
plan-cache-hit engine path, and these two blocks are where it shows; neither half generalises to

### The per-change figures


Everything in this section is **a single commit's own interleaved A/B on the shape it names**, with a
noise floor measured first and the build mode stated where it matters. **None of it is a
release-level claim and none may be read as one.** The full itemisation, with every cost as well as
every gain, is in [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md#performance).

Three things bound every figure below:

- **The host is a 10-core Apple M4 with 4 performance and 6 efficiency cores** (#2691 measured the
  split; `runtime.NumCPU` and `GOMAXPROCS` both report 10 and neither predicts it). Every reading in
  the campaign that treated the machine as ten uniform cores **mis-located the scaling region** —
  "the 1..8 region is the scaling signal" was wrong, because 8 is already past the peak on a
  read-only Cypher workload, which knees at 3–4.
- **Several arms are laboratory ceilings, not user-facing queries.** `index-count-hot` drives one
  counter on one cache line; `index-hash-rw` and `index-btree-rw` drive an index directly. They

  identical source (#2716). Every allocation figure here states its build mode, and the gates that
  pin them carry two build-tagged constants rather than papering over the difference.

### Two results reported as failures of their own criterion

Neither is dressed up, and both are more useful than the number they were chasing.

**A 95.4 % cut in mutex delay moved throughput not at all** (#2710). Settling a property delete's
three non-mutating outcomes under the shared lock took `delNodePropertyInfo`'s mutex delay from
153.03 s to 7.03 s on spreads that do not overlap, and both sites combined fell 64.5 %. Throughput at
64 goroutines rose 9.2 %, which does not clear the cell's published ±11.5 % and does not clear the
re-measured A-vs-A floor of **±32.8 %** for that specific cell either (t=1.98, p≈0.07). **The lock
was never the limiter here: removing the waiting does not remove the work.** And the finding that
outranks the fix is that the `@64` cell was measuring **harness cross-talk** rather than engine write
scaling — a shared Bolt-probe fixture with a fixed label and id against one shared server, giving a
**283× amplification** between 5.6 and 1 595 engine calls per operation for an identical 2 000
operations. That is #2728, and a published 0.453 for that cell is not a clean engine number.

**Widening the write barrier was the task, and the barrier holds zero delay** (#2697). The round-2
inventory ranked `execUnderBarrier` at 72–99 % of write-path blocked time. The **flat** column reads
0 and 0 % on all three write workloads while the **cumulative** column reads 84.90 %, 89.62 % and
99.08 %. Cumulative attribution locates a subsystem; it does not say where goroutines block, because
a parent frame inherits everything beneath it and `execUnderBarrier` wraps the entire write path.
**A cumulative share is not a bottleneck.** No behavioural change shipped; what landed instead is a
godoc naming each ACID guarantee the bracket is credited with against the mechanism that actually
supplies it. A second hypothesis fell with it: sharding `propMapShards` 64 → 256 buys 2–3 % against a
measured 3.69 % noise floor, and the write path already runs at 649–669 % of ten nominal cores
against this host's practical 7.61×, so the blocked time is a **symptom of oversubscription, not its
cause**.

## Premises refuted — the negative results

This project treats a refuted premise as a result, not as a failed task. Sprints 352 and 353 produced
an unusual number of them, and burying them would misrepresent what the release cost and what it
learned.

|---|---|

### 1 and 2 — the two durable formats

These two are the reason the release exists in the shape it does, and both were **reproduced through
the public API alone** rather than argued.

**`store/txn` (#2742).** An acknowledged commit silently lost or corrupted data. Driven through
`Tx.AddNode`, `SetNodeLabel`, `Commit`, `wal.Close`, `recovery.Open`: a 65 535-byte node label
round-tripped, a **65 536-byte one committed with `nil` and recovered as 0 bytes**, and a
**70 000-byte one committed with `nil` and recovered as a wrong 4 464 bytes** — 70 000 mod 65 536.
ACID Durability and Consistency. The guard #1903 added for exactly this, in its own words so that a
caller bypassing the DDL boundary "e.g. the embedded Go API" can "never silently truncate a label and
corrupt the WAL", had reached **2 of the 7 encoders**. `store/wal.Encode` additionally gained the
aggregate bound it never had: a `PropList` of a thousand individually-legal 5 MB elements assembles a
5 GB frame that only the framer can see, so a 1–4 GiB frame was written, fsynced and acknowledged
while `Decode` was **required** to refuse it — PostgreSQL's `XLogRecordMaxSize` case verbatim.

**`store/snapshot` (#2743).** The severity was understated in the task, which asked whether the
checkpointer truncates the WAL behind a snapshot its own reader will refuse and said that answer
decides between "recovery fails loudly" and "committed data is gone". **It is the second, and worse
than either branch contemplated: loud failure *and* unrecoverable loss.** Verified line by line:
phase-1 capture accepted the oversize field with no error; phase 2 published and fsynced a CRC-valid
snapshot; phase 3's self-sufficiency gate matched file **names** and never opened `properties.bin`,
never attempted a readback, and returned true; and the checkpointer then discarded the WAL prefix. On
restart `ReadProperties` refused and the store never opened again, with the WAL that held the data
gone. Post-fix the refusal lands in phase 1 **under the commit lock, before any file is written**, so
the outcome is a failed checkpoint and a **retained** WAL.

**The caps go writer-to-reader, not the other way, and the reasoning is recorded** because the other
direction is worse rather than merely different: the reader caps **are** the anti-OOM control — the
code says so, naming a hostile `stringCount` and a ~16 GiB allocation — so raising them to the
writer's 4 GiB would mean `make([]byte, n)` up to 4 GiB on an untrusted file, the memory-DoS class
this project already carries a recorded HIGH finding for. That trades a durability bug for a security
regression. The usual objection to lowering does not apply here: **the write was never a working
feature, it was a trap that destroyed the WAL.**

### 4, 5 and 6 — the three `graph/lpg` life-record repairs

All three are ACID violations found by the DST and diagnosed together in #2723.

**A refused removal must report its refusal, or its undo resurrects the entity** (#2725 for edges,
#2726 for nodes). `cypher/api.go` pre-probed with `HasEdge` / `IsTombstoned`, called a **void**
primitive whose refusal was therefore invisible, and journalled the undo inverse off the **probe**
rather than the **outcome**. A removal refused by an already-doomed transaction mutated nothing, and
that transaction's rollback then *created* the entity: an arc no committed transaction ever made, or
a bare unnamed node the workload never creates. Making the primitives return their outcome is the fix
at the layer that knows.

Why the obvious reproduction fails, and it explains the counters: two transactions racing to delete
the same node passes 8/8, because whichever lands first tombstones it and the loser's own probe then
reads `wasLive == false` and correctly suppresses the inverse. **The defect needs the node still live
at the refusal, which only an already-doomed transaction produces.**

**A rolled-back `DELETE` destroyed a node's committed birth** (#2724). Three facts composed it: the
life store is one record deep per direction, so a write displaces what was there; a `Rollback`
publishes a **real** instant rather than `AbortedTS`, so the undo replay's revive overwrote the
committed birth; and `aliveBefore` — the #2445 repair — is a *sequence* comparison that masked the
loss only while the died half was still the rollback's own. A later, unrelated delete replaced it, the
pair flipped to born-then-died, and `NodeExistsAsOf` returned **false** for every reader older than
the rollback. The reader stayed correct **through** the rollback itself and lost the node the instant
any subsequent delete was merely pending: a dirty read and a snapshot move at once.

A dead end is recorded so it is not re-attempted blind: extending the fix with the displaced birth's
instant, to also serve readers pinned *before* a node's creation, **reintroduced the defect** on crash
seed 932, 10/10 deterministic. Closing that question requires a record-layout change, which is a
design decision and not a task's to take.

### 7 — `EXPLAIN` and `PROFILE` in Cypher

The plan renderers were reachable only from Go, so a Cypher client — every Bolt driver included —
could not ask for a plan at all, and the gap was recorded as a known driver-compatibility hole leaving
`ResultSummary.Plan()`/`Profile()` nil.

`EXPLAIN` returns the statement's own column signature with **zero rows** and does not execute;
`PROFILE` returns the query's real rows. **The plan is not rows**: it travels beside the result —
`Result.Plan()` / `Result.Profile()` in Go, the `plan`/`profile` fields of the terminal Bolt
`SUCCESS` — which is where `ResultSummary` reads it from, and at most one is ever populated so
estimates cannot be mistaken for measurements. Field names and types were transcribed from the
driver's own decoder rather than recalled: `time` is emitted as `int64` because the driver asserts it
unchecked and a float would panic it, and the page-cache fields are **omitted rather than zeroed**,
since this engine has no page cache and reporting 0 would be a measurement claim it cannot make.
Driver-compat rises **28 → 30 of 37**.

Two limitations are documented rather than hidden. `PROFILE` refuses a **writing** statement, because
the profiling wrapper is installed by the read builder — identical to `Engine.Profile`'s existing
refusal, so the Cypher and Go surfaces agree. And neither prefix may precede a schema statement, which
the hand-written DDL parser handles outside this grammar: a prefixed one is a syntax error and
therefore executes nothing, which is the property that matters.

Both words remain usable as identifiers outside prefix position, and that was **falsified rather than
assumed**: removing the `symbol`-rule alternatives and regenerating makes all seven cases of
`TestPlanPrefix_IdentifiersSurviveTokenisation` fail, one with a parser panic on
`MATCH (explain) RETURN explain`.

## Behavioural changes that will surprise

None of these needs a code change to compile. Each is something a running system could observe.

- **A query that used to return extra rows now returns the right ones.** Untyped `<-[r]-` or `-[r]-`
  under `EXISTS`, `COUNT { }`, a pattern predicate, or any correlated `Apply` no longer returns rows
  for outer nodes with no matching edge (#2251). The **typed** spelling was accidentally correct
  before, so only the untyped one changes.
- **A `SET` on a write-clause-bound relationship now lands.** `MERGE (a)-[r:T]->(b) SET r.since =
  2020` left `r.since` null — silently, with `PropertiesSet` counting the write (#2705). Plain
  `CREATE (a)-[r:T]->(b) SET r.k`, `MERGE`'s `MATCH` branch, `SET r += {…}`, `SET r = {…}`,
  `WITH r SET` and `REMOVE r.k` were all affected. **Relationship identity changes with it:** two
  parallel `CREATE`s between the same ordered pair previously published the same id, so `r1 = r2` was
  true for two distinct relationships and `DISTINCT` collapsed them.
- **A correlated `EXISTS` after a write clause is correlated again** (#2659). A statement of the shape
  `MATCH … CREATE … WITH a WHERE EXISTS { MATCH (a)-[:Z]->(:P) }` returned null rows that should have
  been filtered out; `NOT EXISTS` failed in the opposite direction.
- **`PROFILE` renders the plan that actually runs, and its root row count now equals the query's**
  (#2665). If you were reading `PROFILE` output to decide which plan the engine picked, the answer
  changes for every shape that reaches the columnar chain or the cyclic-intersect fusion.
- **`PROFILE`'s db-hits changed on three families** (#2720). `VarLengthExpand` is now **measured**
  rather than derived and can report a figure **202×** higher on a fan-out; a type-filtered `Expand`
  is unchanged but was previously **100×** low; and the three parallel leaves now render
  `parallel tier; db-hits not counted` rather than `0`. A dashboard or regression test asserting the
  old numbers will move.
- **`EXPLAIN` now carries its plan-time notifications** (#2721) — it was dropping them, 0 against 1 on
  a Cartesian product.
- **`ORDER BY` ties are ordered by arrival** (#2509). `Top` previously let the heap's sift sequence
  decide the position of tied rows. Invisible for `LIMIT` alone; visible for `SKIP … LIMIT`, where it
  changed **which rows the page contains**.
- **Deleted nodes are no longer briefly visible to present-time readers** (#2687), and a reopened
  store no longer serves deleted nodes from `MATCH (n:L)` — that second one is the **recovery** shape,
  because recovery applies snapshot tombstones before snapshot labels.
- **An exact label count is snapshot-stable** (#2688). A read transaction previously observed two
  different counts, and could observe a **partially applied** transaction: the values 481, 526 and 266
  are not multiples of the 5 nodes each transaction commits.
- **`store/csrfile.BuildFixture` returns an error where it used to panic** (#2744), and refuses a
  `Vertices` count above `MaxUint32` that previously produced a **silently wrong graph with no error
  at all**. The signature is unchanged — it already returned `(*csr.CSR[struct{}], error)`.
- **`cmd/sim` refuses a seedless invocation with exit 2** (#2663). It previously drew a seed from the
  auto-seeded top-level generator, so two identical commands ran two different simulations and a
  violation could not be reproduced from its own report.
- **`bolt/server` publishes thirteen new metric series** (#2715), one latency histogram per Bolt
  message type, emitted from `Session.HandleMessage`. A scrape's series count rises.
- **The documented graph configuration for Cypher and Bolt is now
  `adjlist.Config{Directed: true, Multigraph: true, Weightless: true}`** (#2651). `Multigraph` is the
  important half: openCypher's model **is** a multigraph, and a bare `adjlist.Config{}` — which the
  package godoc showed — is neither directed nor a multigraph, so a second `CREATE` between the same
  ordered pair failed with `ErrParallelEdgeInSimpleGraph`. This is **guidance, not a module default**:
  `bolt/server` constructs no graph, the caller always supplies one.
- **`cypher.EngineOptions.EdgeTypeFilterCacheCapacity` and `DefaultEdgeTypeFilterCacheCapacity` are
  deprecated** (#2251). Both are inert on every path and claim no replacement, because the
  relationship-type column that replaced the cache is type-set independent and there is nothing left
  to size.

- **No dependency migration.** `go.mod` and `go.sum` are **byte-identical** to `v0.12.0`: same
  `toolchain go1.27.0`, same `go 1.26` directive, same versions of all nineteen modules. Nothing is
  required of you and nothing is claimed.
- **No on-disk migration, in either direction.** The snapshot manifest stays at version 3 and
  `csrfile` at version 1. A `v0.12.0` store opens on `v0.13.0` and a `v0.13.0` store opens on
  `v0.12.0`. What changed is that `v0.13.0` **refuses to write** a field that `v0.12.0` would have
  written unreadably — so an artefact written by `v0.13.0` is, if anything, safer to open with an
  older reader than one written by `v0.12.0`.
- **Two things are worth doing on upgrade, neither required.** If you write very large labels,
  property keys or property values, **check your `Commit` error for `txn.ErrFieldTooLong`** and your
  checkpoint error for `snapshot.ErrFieldTooLong`; before #2742 and #2743 an over-long field was
  truncated or written unreadably, and after them it is refused. And if you scrape GoGraph's
  Prometheus endpoint, **budget for thirteen new Bolt latency series** (#2715).
- **Read [Behavioural changes that will surprise](#behavioural-changes-that-will-surprise)** before
  deploying, particularly if you read `PROFILE` output programmatically, rely on `ORDER BY` tie order
  with `SKIP`, or assert on db-hits.

## Known issues

The costs listed in [What got slower](#what-got-slower--measured-not-minimised) are all deliberate
trades, each measured and each stated with its own figure. They are not defects and none is filed for
recovery. Beyond them, the following are open findings recorded in this window's commit record, with
the evidence that raised them.

| id | Area | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| **#2675** | cypher | **A wrong answer openCypher requires otherwise, and the TCK cannot see it.** `ir.TranslateSubquery` builds a subquery's inner plan from `q.ReadingClauses` alone, so a body's **final projection is never translated**: a `RETURN`'s `DISTINCT`, `ORDER BY`, `SKIP`, `LIMIT` or aggregation is discarded, and `COUNT { MATCH … RETURN count(*) }` answers **2 where 1 is correct**. Found in sprint 352, **not fixed in this window**, and pre-existing rather than introduced by it. The blindness was verified independently: **zero of 220 feature files contain `COUNT {`**, so the conformance count cannot move on it in either direction. `cypher/count_subquery_block_form_test.go` records the gap in its own godoc and deliberately asserts no absolute value for the two cases it reaches. |
| **#2749** | store/checkpoint | **The checkpoint gate never verifies readability at all.** #2743 closed one path — an over-long field — but `snapshotIsSelfSufficient` still judges a snapshot by matching file **names** in the manifest and never opens a component or attempts a readback. The largest of sprint 353's 21 follow-ups. |
| **#2740** | cypher/parser | The parser holds **97.54 % of read-path mutex delay at 1024 goroutines**. |
| **#2732** | cypher | Whole-node materialisation is **55 % of read allocations**. |
| **#2734** | cypher | On an **undirected** engine, `DETACH DELETE` writes **2× the WAL frames it needs** — a fan-out of 64 writes 128 frames for 64 removed edges, 48.7 % of the delete transaction's WAL bytes. Not fixed, because `adjlist.Config{}` defaults to `Directed: false` while every production-shaped wiring passes `Directed: true`, and whether Cypher over an undirected LPG is a supported configuration at all is unsettled — that question decides the fix. |
| **#2753** | test | Opened at sprint-353 close for `cypher.TestReadPathAllocationCeiling`, which read exactly 20.00 in **every** isolated configuration tried — plain, `-race`, `-cover`, `-coverpkg` with atomic, parallel suite, whole package, `GOGC` off/400/1, and 30 consecutive runs — and exactly 32.00 under the full concurrent module run. Six hypotheses were refuted and the cause was recorded as **not established**; the sprint was merged red on the user's instruction. The **final commit on the sprint branch then diagnosed it** (#2751): `testing.AllocsPerRun` reads `runtime.MemStats.Mallocs`, which is **process-global**, so the 12 extra objects were entirely other goroutines. The gate now takes the minimum of five and reads exactly 20.00. **The two records disagree and both are in the tree** — see [What this release does not establish](#what-this-release-does-not-establish). #2753's own first requirement, to settle whether the behaviour predates the sprint, is not answered here. |

fixed**.

## What this release does not establish

This section is part of the release, not a footnote to it.

1. **This release is not certified.** The most recent production certification is
   [`docs/certification-2026-08-13.md`](../docs/certification-2026-08-13.md), taken at the `v0.11.0`
   release commit — now **291 commits** behind this tag. No certification was run in this window.
   Everything the `v0.11.0` envelope did not establish, it still does not.
2. **The whole-tree soak layer was not run** — a sixth consecutive cycle. The artefacts in
   `soak-artefacts/` are from **2026-05-30 at commit `b5453b9`**. Gate 4 of `1.0.0` is not discharged.
3. **Latency percentiles at the published concurrency levels were not measured as a release
   deliverable.** Individual commits report p50/p99 for the specific arms they moved — #2681's
   `162.4 µs → 10.6 µs` at 1024, #2682's `1 001.2 µs → 0.2 µs`, #2693's `31.7 µs → 13.4 µs` — but
   there is no whole-module p50/p99 table at 1/8/64/256/1024, for a fifth consecutive cycle.
4. **Every performance figure in this release comes from one host, one architecture and one core
   topology** — an Apple M4, 10 cores, 4 performance + 6 efficiency, `darwin/arm64`, **go1.27.1** (the pinned `toolchain` directive is go1.27.0; Go uses the newer local toolchain). Several
   results depend on that topology explicitly and say so: `shardCount = 32` in `internal/metrics` is
   tuned for this host and a much larger machine should re-run `BenchmarkIncCounterParallel` before
   assuming it holds (#2698); the read-path knee at 3–4 goroutines is the **performance**-core count
   (#2691); and the practical scaling ceiling measured here is **7.61×**, not 10× (#2679).
5. **No container was ever run**, and no Linux, no NUMA, no multi-socket.
6. **Only one section of this document carries release-level figures.** The `v0.12.0` → `v0.13.0`
   comparison is confined to
   [*What this release delivers over `v0.12.0`*](#what-this-release-delivers-over-v0120-measured-at-both-trees);
   its method, per-block tables and raw `benchstat` output live in
   [`docs/benchmarks/v0.13.0.md`](../docs/benchmarks/v0.13.0.md). **Every other figure in this
   document is a single commit's own local A/B on the shape it names** and must not be read as a
   release-level delta — including the large per-change numbers under *Highlights*, which are the
   ones most likely to be quoted out of context.
7. **Several arms in `bench/contention` establish nothing and are labelled as such.** Five ceiling
   arms' level-1 cells sit inside their own spread, so raw and normalised are equally good and the
   inventory says so rather than publishing a falsely precise number (#2712). Four causes in that
   document are labelled **hypotheses** rather than asserted.
8. **Part of the earlier contention record could not be verified and is stated as such.** A freshness
   audit scanned every surviving file on the host with a `bench/contention` package result line — 312
   files, 327 lines (#2713). Round 1's scaling table and sites 1–8 and round 2's ranked inventory are
   **verified fresh** by four independent lines each. Round 1's site-3 follow-ups and label-index
   ceiling, and round 2's fault-rate and error-class probes, seed replays, cross-reference counts and
   core-utilisation figures **could not be verified**: no surviving artefact contains them. An honest
   "cannot be verified" is the correct answer where the evidence is gone, and the sweeps were **not**
   re-run to replace them.
9. **`bolt-tx-read`, `bolt-tx-read-noquota` and `bolt-wire-read-metrics` share the transport cause
   #2711 quantified, with unknown magnitude**, and `bolt-connect-churn` is excluded because it dials
   per operation so not even the sign is predictable.
10. **The 69 new public-API identifiers were not exercised by an external consumer.** They are covered
    by the module's own tests, the DST and the TCK; no downstream integration exists.
11. **The sprint-353 close record and its branch tip disagree about `make ci`, and both are in the
    tree.** The merge commit records `cypher.TestReadPathAllocationCeiling` as a **known red**, merged
    deliberately on the user's instruction with the cause **not established** and tracked as #2753;
    the final commit on the branch it merges (#2751, four hours earlier) diagnoses the cause as
    process-global `runtime.MemStats.Mallocs` contamination, makes the gate take the minimum of five,
    and reports it reading exactly 20.00. The most likely reading is that the merge message was drafted
    from the sprint record before the last commit landed, but **that is an inference and it is not
    claimed as fact**. Whichever it is, the authoritative statement for this release is the one
    `VERSION=v0.13.0 make release-preflight` produces at the release candidate.
12. **#2675 is a known openCypher divergence shipping open**, and the TCK is structurally blind to it.
    A subquery body's final projection is never translated, so `COUNT { MATCH … RETURN count(*) }`
    answers 2 where 1 is correct. It is pre-existing rather than introduced here, and the 3897/3897
    figure this release reports **cannot** move on it in either direction. See
    [Known issues](#known-issues).
