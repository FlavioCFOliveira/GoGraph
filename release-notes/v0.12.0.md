## Overview

**192 commits** — 87 fixes, 51 features, 25 documentation, 18 test, 4 performance,
1 style, 1 chore, 1 build change, and 4 merges. **Six sprints delivered work in this
window — 345 to 350 — and 176 tasks closed.**

This is the fifteenth published release of **GoGraph**, a Go module for graph
persistence, manipulation and fast search. It is a pre-1.0 **MINOR** release, and it is
the opposite kind of release from `v0.11.0`. Where `v0.11.0` replaced the module's
concurrency-control mechanism and shipped 31 breaking changes, `v0.12.0` **adds no
engine capability and breaks nothing**. It is a testing-and-correctness release.

Two things define it.

**A deterministic-simulation-testing campaign drove every published surface.** Sprints
345 to 349 — 110 tasks — took the DST harness in `internal/sim` from a
graph-and-storage exerciser to one that drives MVCC under true concurrency, the full
Cypher language surface, the storage/durability/MVCC substrate, the Bolt wire, and the
graph API, search and bulk-load surfaces. **50 of the release's 51 `feat` commits are
`feat(sim)`.** This is test infrastructure, and it is worth saying plainly rather than
dressing it up: `internal/sim` accounts for **127 267 of the 168 208 changed lines
(75.7 %)**, while the module's own production code accounts for **8 564 lines across 93
files (5.1 %)**.

**And the bug backlog was emptied.** Sprint 350 — 66 tasks — resolved every known bug,
re-validating each premise at HEAD before working it. No `BUG`-type task remains in the
backlog.

Between them, **50 defects in the shipped module were found and fixed**: 26 across
sprints 345 to 349 and 24 in sprint 350. Five of the severity-9 ones were **silent** —
wrong or lost data with no error anywhere:

| Defect | What a user would have seen |
|---|---|
| **#2526** | Every edge weight of a non-primitive type — a `time.Duration`, a struct — **silently lost at the first checkpoint**, permanently. 95 of 95 weights present at the WAL boundary, 0 of 191 after one checkpoint. Bulk import was worse: it bypasses the WAL, so no surviving copy existed anywhere. |
| **#2603** | `MATCH (:Person)-[:KNOWS]->(:Vip) RETURN count(*)` returned **0** where the named-source spelling returned 1, on the shipped default configuration. |
| **#2504** | Reverse and undirected traversal over a reciprocal edge pair returned **the other edge's** properties, type, `startNode` and `endNode`, while `id(r)` stayed correct. |
| **#2513** | **Every** Cypher list crossing the Bolt wire reached clients as a PackStream **String** — `collect()`, `labels()`, `keys()`, `nodes(p)`, `relationships(p)`, list literals and list-valued properties. |
| **#2446** | A write transaction's own uncommitted view leaked through the engine-scoped CSR and edge-type-filter caches, so **committed edges lost their types for every reader** at that instant. |

**The most consequential result of the campaign is not a scenario but a control.** Until
rmp #2535, `SimDisk` retained unsynced file data across a simulated host crash, so the
crash battery **could not fail an engine that stopped calling `fsync` at all**. With a
durable-length watermark in place, deleting the WAL commit fsync now fails loudly:
`checkpoint-crash-storm` reports **192 violations at seed 610621185, losing exactly half
of 384 acknowledged commits, and 33 top-level tests fail**, where before the change it
failed nothing. Every durability claim this release makes about the simulator rests on
that control existing.

**Nothing breaks, and there is nothing to migrate.** See
[Upgrade notes](#upgrade-notes). A dozen observable behaviours *did* change, and they
are listed in [Behavioural changes that will surprise](#behavioural-changes-that-will-surprise).

## Semantic-versioning rationale

`v0.12.0` is a pre-1.0 **MINOR** release. Two facts decide it, and both were established
by audit rather than asserted:

1. **No breaking change.** No commit in `v0.11.0..HEAD` carries a `!` marker or a
   `BREAKING CHANGE` footer — 0 of 192. An audit of the whole-tree diff for removed
   exported declarations found **no exported identifier removed, no exported signature
   changed, and no exported struct field dropped**. Every removal the audit surfaced is
   either a `Test*` function or lives under `internal/`, which
   [docs/semver.md](../docs/semver.md) excludes from the public API.
2. **New exported identifiers were added** — **51** across 15 packages, which
   [docs/semver.md](../docs/semver.md) makes a MINOR bump. The largest groups are
   `store/snapshot` (9), `store/recovery` (8), `cypher/exec` (8) and `cypher` (7).

On-disk formats moved **additively, with no version step**, which the same policy also
places at MINOR: the snapshot manifest stays at version 3 while gaining a CRC32C framing
trailer and an `indexes_commit_ts` field, and `csrfile` stays at version 1 while gaining
wire values 5 and 6. Old files open; older readers refuse a new `csrfile` weight kind
rather than misreading it, and skip the manifest trailer entirely.

**Why this is not `v1.0.0`.** The gate status is unchanged from `v0.11.0`: **three of
the four documented `1.0.0` gates are met** — execution-level TCK is at 100 % against a
≥ 95 % requirement, every `T-` divergence is resolved, and the full local gate is green
at the release tree. **Gate 4 is not met**, for a fifth consecutive cycle: it requires
the soak report in `soak-artefacts/` to reflect a run against the release commit, and
those artefacts are from **2026-05-30 at commit `b5453b9`**. That gate, together with
[what this release does not establish](#what-this-release-does-not-establish), is what
stands between GoGraph and `1.0.0`.

## Highlights

### The crash model could not fail an engine that stopped calling fsync

`internal/sim`'s `SimDisk` is what every durability claim in the DST rests on, and an
audit of it — [`docs/audits/simdisk-crash-model-fidelity-2026-08-18.md`](../docs/audits/simdisk-crash-model-fidelity-2026-08-18.md),
**15 findings, 12 filed as tasks** — found that it retained unsynced file data across a
simulated host crash. A model that keeps data the real kernel would drop cannot
distinguish an engine that fsyncs from one that does not.

Six tasks rebuilt it (#2514, #2535, #2536, #2537, #2538, #2547): a rename undo log with
durable-prefix semantics, a **`durableLen` watermark** so unsynced data is now discarded
on a host crash, `CrashHost` and `CrashProcess` split apart, unlink modelled as a
non-durable directory mutation, a `DirSync` fault arm, and ghost directory entries
pruned.

The result is the non-vacuity proof quoted in the [Overview](#overview): with the WAL
commit fsync deleted, `checkpoint-crash-storm` reports **192 violations at seed
610621185, losing exactly half of 384 acknowledged commits**, and 33 top-level tests
fail — where before the change it failed nothing. The watermark's own cost was measured
against the obvious alternative: **+0.00 % bytes and allocations per operation**, against
+97.64 % time and +99.94 % bytes for a full clone. The whole catalogue then passes under
`SOAK_FULL=1` with **36 of 36 scenarios and zero skips**.

### A silent, permanent data loss on every non-primitive edge weight

`store/snapshot` sized weights statically and returned zero for anything outside a fixed
set of Go primitives, so for a graph weighted by a struct or by a **named integer type
such as `time.Duration`** it wrote `hasWeights=0` — **byte-identical to the encoding a
deliberately weightless graph produces** — and the checkpoint then truncated the WAL
prefix still holding the true values. Nothing errored. The recovered graph was
indistinguishable from one that never had any weights.

Measured by the codec matrix (#2473): **95 of 95** weights present at the WAL boundary,
**0 of 191** after one folding checkpoint. Bulk import carried the identical defect and
worse, because it bypasses the WAL, so **no surviving copy existed anywhere**.

The fix (#2526) is a sentinel in the existing width byte selecting a variable-width
section — offsets plus payload — encoded through the store's own weight codec. Offsets
rather than length prefixes, because the apply path **skips** slots whose endpoints
cannot be resolved and a self-delimiting concatenation would desynchronise. But the
encoding is the less important half: **the decision is made before the header is
written**, and the refusal propagates so the checkpoint returns before reaching the
prefix truncation. The one surviving copy is never destroyed. Reads are symmetric — a
missing codec refuses rather than applying zeros over weights that are on disk.

Measured after: a duration weight **0 of 3 → 3 of 3**, and the simulator's
binary-marshaler arm **0 of 17 → 17 of 17** through *snapshot-only* recovery. That last
figure is now a gate, because a codec that survives WAL replay and loses everything
through the snapshot shows a healthy total and a zero there — which is exactly how this
hid. Primitive snapshots stay byte-identical and keep opening on older builds.

### A wrong answer on the shipped default configuration

`MATCH (:Person)-[:KNOWS]->(:Vip) RETURN count(*)` returned **0** where the named-source
spelling returned 1, with no error and no notification (#2603). openCypher requires the
rows: `cypher/tck/features/clauses/match/Match2.feature` scenario [2] pins
`MATCH (:A)-[r]->(:B) RETURN r` to one row.

The mechanism is why the same pattern is correct *without* the anchor-swap peephole. The
written plan enforces the head's label **structurally**, in the access path —
`NodeByLabelScan{fromVar, fromLabel}` scans a label and needs no variable name to do it.
The mirror **relocates** that label onto a `Selection{LabelPredicate(fromVar, …)}` above
the re-rooted expand, and a predicate can only reach a node **through its name**.
`ir.matchNodeScan` leaves the pattern *head*'s `NodeVar` empty while every non-head
anonymous node is given a synthetic `__anon_N`, so the relocated receiver resolved to no
column, `evalLabelPredicate` returned NULL, and `Filter` dropped every row.

Mis-resolution is ruled out rather than assumed: the fixture's destination is
`(:Person:Vip)`, so had `Variable{""}` fallen back to column 0 the `(:Person)` predicate
would have been TRUE and the row kept. It was dropped.

**Why nothing caught it** is the part worth reading. The TCK scenario cannot reach the
peephole for two independent reasons, each sufficient — its relationship is untyped while
`matchAnchorSite` requires exactly one type, and its graph is balanced so the 2× cost
margin is unreachable, both confirmed by `anchorSwapBuildCount` reading 0. And the
optimisation's own tests were blind by construction: all 24 patterns in
`anchor_swap_diff_test.go` and `anchor_swap_symmetric_test.go` used **named** variables
and never `count(*)`, so their differential oracle only ever compared the spelling that
was already right. That gap is closed with anonymous-endpoint arms, which matters more
than the fix.

**The cost is large and is not called negligible.** See
[What got slower](#what-got-slower--measured-not-minimised).

### The v0.11.0 open regression is closed

`v0.11.0` shipped with rmp #2431 open at severity 7: on 100 000 `:Common` nodes of which
1 000 also carry `:Rare`, `MATCH (n:Common:Rare) RETURN n.k` took **4.611 ms in the
default configuration against 0.186 ms** for the plan the same engine already knew how to
build — and worse than the 4.280 ms legacy full-`:Common` scan the re-anchor was written
to replace.

**Which label the gate judged was proved, not read.** Holding `|Rare|` at 1 000 and
sweeping `|Common|`, the plan flips at exactly `|Common| > 50 000` — the parallel
threshold — while the re-anchored label never moves. The bitmap intersection is confirmed
**not** implicated: disabling it changed neither plan nor time.

Root cause: the columnar chain's yield was **inert**. `tryBuildColumnarFilterChain`
already declines when `pickMinLabel` would fire, and states the rule — the re-anchor
reduces the rows *scanned*, while columnar execution only removes a constant factor from
each scanned row, so it must never pre-empt it. `tryBuildParallelScanProject` was tried
immediately after and was not making the same argument, so it claimed every shape the
yield gave up, anchored on `Labels[0]`.

The fix **adopts** the anchor rather than merely declining: the gate asks `pickMinLabel`
and, when it would fire, uses the chosen label as both the row source and the threshold
input. Declining outright would have regressed the case where the smallest label is
itself above the threshold; adopting it makes that case **faster**, because the parallel
scan now partitions the smaller label. Full figures in
[What got faster](#what-got-faster).

### Six search entry points ignored a dead context

`flow.PushRelabelMaxFlowCtx` returned the **true, complete maximum flow with a nil
error** under an already-cancelled context, measured at `MaxNodeID` up to 1536 (#2593). A
request-scoped deadline fires, the caller's context is dead, the library does the full
work and reports success, and nothing downstream can tell.

One root cause behind five of the six: **increment-then-mask**, so the counter is 1 on
the first iteration and the mask first trips at 4096 units of work — any input below that
stride returned a complete answer under a dead context. Measured with a pre-cancelled
context at 8 nodes, every one of `BellmanFordCtx`, `BellmanFordInto`, `KCoreCtx`,
`KShortestPathsLooplessCtx`, `KShortestPathsLooplessCtxWithOpts`, `EppsteinKShortestCtx`
and `PushRelabelMaxFlowCtx` returned nil with a complete result. `TopologicalSortCtx` was
a different mechanism: on a fully cyclic graph every vertex has indegree ≥ 1, so the
polled Kahn loop never runs and `ErrCycle` was returned in preference to the context
error, at every input size.

**The count is closed by scan rather than by trust.** A detector for the shape, run over
all **47 stride-gated polls** in the module, leaves exactly two increment-then-mask
sites, both benign because their callers poll unconditionally first. And **no
live-context result changed, which is measured rather than argued: 3 622 signatures
byte-identical before and after** — 3 025 over the Bellman-Ford, k-core, topological-sort
and k-shortest families across `n` in {1,2,3,8,64,1500,4097,5000}, 57 flow signatures
including a fingerprint of the mutated residual network, and 540 Johnson signatures at
two resolutions.

## The DST campaign, sprint by sprint

The five DST sprints are the bulk of the release. Each entry separates **what the harness
gained** from **what it found in the module**, because only the second is a change a user
consumes.

### Sprint 345 — MVCC and true concurrent production scenarios (13 tasks)

**The harness gained a second execution mode.** A deterministic, seed-reproducible
multi-session mode in which K logical `cypher.Session` clients run explicit
multi-statement transactions interleaved at **statement** granularity on one goroutine
over the WAL-backed store (`RunMVCCSessions`), plus `OracleTx`, a per-transaction
workspace over the graph oracle with BEGIN-snapshot semantics and a validate-then-apply
atomic fold. Three MVCC isolation checkers — snapshot stability, read-your-own-writes
with doomed-transaction adjudication, and atomic visibility (#2436). A lost-update and
write-conflict adjudication scenario exact at transaction granularity and requiring typed
refusals through `errors.Is` (#2437). Crash injection **while explicit MVCC transactions
are open**, adjudicated at transaction granularity (#2438). In the genuinely concurrent
Bolt-wire mode: transactional writer roles with a five-bucket per-connection ledger
(#2439) and three during-run isolation oracles (#2440). All combined into a named
`production-profile` scenario with crash cycles (#2441).

**It found four module defects, all in the MVCC substrate**, and the multi-session mode
found three of them on its first day:

- **#2443** (severity 8) — two root causes. `NodeExistsAsOf` answered TRUE for a node
  whose birth *and* death records were both invisible, so a create+delete inside one open
  transaction showed concurrent readers a **bare phantom node** with no labels or
  properties. And `reclaimAbortedLife` treated an aborted birth+death pair as independent
  directions — tombstone, then revive, revive winning — so a conflict-doomed
  transaction's rolled-back creates **leaked as persistent phantoms**.
- **#2444** (severity 9) — a `DETACH DELETE` of a node carrying another in-flight
  transaction's pending property write **sailed through** where a second property write
  is refused, and its eager present-state mutations then destroyed state a rollback could
  not restore: with both transactions rolled back, the committed node was **gone from the
  label scan permanently**. The node is now the unit of write–write conflict: every side
  claims in its own store first and cross-checks the others after, so whichever claim
  lands second sees the other and detection is race-free without a per-node lock. Edge
  appends check and stamp **both** endpoints, directed graphs included, as Memgraph
  prepares both vertices.
- **#2445** (severity 9) — four defects, each with a minimal deterministic reproduction
  that fails on the unfixed engine. The life store's chain-level primordial flag misread
  a died+born pair written over committed history, so a doomed rollback re-tombstoned the
  node its undo had revived and a **voluntary** rollback — which publishes its inverses —
  made the node invisible to readers pinned before it. The undo replay's own-arc
  adjacency withdrawals were refused when a foreign commuting append had moved the head,
  **leaking the rolled-back edge as a torn arc**. Adjacency entries are immutable
  snapshots built from the current slot, so a concurrent append embedded another
  transaction's pending arc. And `addEdgeInfo`/`addEdgeHInfo` ran the #2444 endpoint
  cross-check *after* the physical insert and returned the refusal with the arc still in
  the slot, so the next committed append published the phantom arc.
- **#2446** (severity 9) — the engine-scoped CSR pair cache and edge-type-filter cache
  key on `(epoch, startTS)` with **no transaction identity**, while the pair is built
  as-of `(startTS, snap.TxID())`. A write transaction querying after its own edge create
  cached a CSR containing its own pending arc, and every reader at the same instant was
  served that private view: CSR positions shifted, the position-keyed type filter
  mislanded, and **committed edges lost their types for every reader**. Found as seed
  16's typed-count flip at the exact tick of an unrelated pending create.

### Sprint 346 — the full Cypher language surface (28 tasks)

**The harness became a Cypher-language oracle.** Seventeen capability tasks: access-path
parity and plan-stability oracles that compare literal against `$param` arms for
identical multisets *and* identical access-path leaf operators from `Explain`, with a
`MustSeek` vacuity guard (#2447); a per-tick `QueryCounters` oracle comparing all twelve
counter fields against the exact delta the oracle predicts, with zero extra queries
(#2448); multigraph mode and parallel-edge instance pinning (#2449); index seeks over
7 arms × literal/`$param` (#2450); a `pattern-shapes` scenario unlocking the
join/intersect planner family (#2451); grouped aggregation with **exact** `avg`,
`stDev`, `stDevP`, `percentileCont` and `percentileDisc` oracle arithmetic (#2452); a
`null-semantics` scenario with genuinely NULL-bearing data so the three-valued-logic
probes stop being tautological (#2453); `FOREACH` write-path equivalence by
openCypher-reference expansion (#2454); schema introspection checked against both the
harness model *and* `db.indexes()`/`db.constraints()` (#2455); the statistics-planning
regime through `db.stats.refresh()`, where statistics may change the plan but never the
result (#2456); genuine temporal binding with **type-kind** read-back assertions, closing
an ISO-string tautology (#2457); the pure-function battery from 90 to **135** absolute-
literal cases (#2458); list and map-valued property predicates (#2459); ordering and
multi-part query structure with a **plan divergence** assertion — the unlimited arm must
plan `Sort` and never `Top` (#2460); the `MERGE` surface with exact counters (#2461); the
`DELETE` contract, `CALL YIELD WHERE`, cartesian notifications and the Bolt wire
parameter type matrix (#2462); and the variable-length path surface against a
trail-enumerating reference (#2463).

Coverage moved where the checkers landed: `graph/index/stats` **0 % → 56.2 %** (#2456),
`cypher/funcs` **41.0 % → 79.8 % of functions** (#2458). At the default seed the VLE
reference computes an anchored closure of 10 177 trails at depth 30 in 251 µs against the
engine's 5.4 ms, **both exact** (#2463).

**It found eleven module defects** — every one in `cypher/` or `bolt/`, and every one
recorded with the TCK held at 3897/3897:

- **The parallel-edge family** (#2500, #2501, #2502, #2503). Relationship `REMOVE`
  reported `PropertiesRemoved=1` only for the *first* removal of a `(src,dst)` pair,
  because both mutator adapters gated the counter on the aggregate pair probe while the
  mutation ran per handle; five more SET-null routes did the same; the `SET r = map`
  replace teardown enumerated only aggregate pair keys so an **instance-only key survived
  a replace** whose contract is "the resulting map equals exactly the given map"; and
  every source-side whole-entity copy read the aggregate map, leaking twin-only keys into
  the copy target — 18 of 18 subtests red on both write paths. #2500 and #2501 were
  report-only defects; #2502 and #2503 returned wrong data.
- **#2504** (severity 9) — reverse and undirected traversal bound the **reciprocal**
  edge's data. The suspected reverse-expansion mapping was **not** the cause: `revtofwd`
  is handle-exact. `exec.Expand` emits its triple in *traversal* order with no direction
  flag, so the hydrator re-derived storage direction by probing existence, which is
  undecidable exactly when both directions exist. A second site — the `WITH`-barrier
  whole-entity projection — was found only after reinstating the simulator arm. The fix
  measured **2.13 % faster**, allocations identical.
- **#2505** (severity 8) — **seven defects, not one.** `UNWIND` over a pattern
  comprehension is not hoisted into `RollUpApply`, so it fell to a fallback evaluator
  that had drifted: the walk advanced to the anchor on a reverse leg; `id(r)` was 0;
  endpoints came back transposed; properties were read on transposed keys **and per
  pair**, so parallel siblings reported the last writer's values; and the type filter was
  per pair too, so `[r:KNOWS]` over a `KNOWS`/`LIKES` pair emitted two rows where `MATCH`
  emits one. Two more were then found via self-loops.
- **#2507** (severity 8) — **six defects, and the reported root cause was wrong.** A
  projection-position `EXISTS { }` / `COUNT { }` with an inner filter was constantly
  false or zero. Instrumenting the inner `Filter` closure showed the node hydrating
  correctly: `StripLiterals` hoists **STRING** literals onto auto-parameters and
  `compileSubAST` passed a nil parameter map, so the auto-parameter evaluated to NULL —
  which is exactly why an age filter always worked and a name filter never did. **Zero
  of 220 TCK feature files use a braced `EXISTS` or `COUNT` subquery.**
- **The `MERGE` action family** (#2510, #2511, #2512). A whole-entity relationship action
  was silently dropped when the endpoints are unbound — **20 of 24 matrix cells**; an
  action targeting a variable bound *outside* the pattern was lost across **30 of 36
  cells** on all three merge operators, and a zero-row driver additionally **panicked**
  on a nil interface dereference; and `MERGE` **fired once and wrote data when its
  driving clause returned zero rows** — 18 cells, across every zero-row shape.
- **#2513** (severity 9) — Bolt encoded every Cypher list as a PackStream String, so
  `collect`, `labels`, `keys`, `nodes` and `relationships` were broken **for all
  drivers**. The record encoder had no list case despite its own godoc claiming one.

### Sprint 347 — storage, durability and the MVCC substrate (34 tasks)

**The largest infrastructure sprint.** It closed the cross-cutting hole that no scenario
occupied concurrent writers *and* a checkpoint *and* a crash simultaneously, and — the
part that matters most — it repaired the fidelity of the crash model itself (see
[Highlights](#the-crash-model-could-not-fail-an-engine-that-stopped-calling-fsync)).

Seventeen capability tasks: a `ddl-checkpoint-crash` scenario (#2464); a crash **during**
checkpoint publish and WAL-prefix truncation, closing the #1827 debt (#2465);
`store/bulkimport` publish under fault with two-sided exact parity (#2466); a
nine-component snapshot-corruption fail-stop battery (#2467); the full typed-value and
edge-property matrix through the snapshot codec (#2468); MVCC clock and
transaction-sequence recovery across a snapshot boundary (#2469); an MVCC substrate
telemetry oracle over chain depth, vacuum watermark and version-memory bound (#2470); a
group-commit coalescing oracle plus its fail-all arm (#2471); WAL writer surface oracles
with per-transaction frame contiguity (#2472); a key and weight codec matrix across crash
and upgrade, six of whose seven pairs write a version-two byte mapper no scenario had
ever produced (#2473); transaction-size caps with producer refusal and replay fail-stop
(#2474); `store.DB` teardown under a cancelled context and 16 racing closers (#2475); the
background checkpointer on a fake clock (#2476); cross-release compatibility beyond the
WAL (#2477, #2531); `csrfile` access patterns — five values, one previously reached by no

  moved inside the package and the omission became inexpressible. **Three shipped

- **#2515** (severity 8) — a node-only `MERGE` with an outer-relationship action

**It found six module defects**, two of them inside capability tasks:

- **#2593** (severity 8) — the six search entry points that ignored a dead context, in

- **#2600** (severity 7) — `graph/query`'s `WithRange` gave different answers from its
  index and its scan for mixed numeric kinds, and the `int64` seek arms were dead. **The
  direction was settled from the project's own conformance authority**, not from memory:
  `Comparison2.feature`'s "Comparing across types yields null, except numbers" sweeps all
  90 ordered cross-type pairs and keeps exactly the four INTEGER/FLOAT rows; `Comparison1`
  has `1 = 1.0` as true; and CIP2016-06-14, adopted and normative, has INTEGER × FLOAT as
  the only off-diagonal entry in its comparability matrix. **So the scan was the defect.**
  The comparison is exact, not a promotion, because `Comparison1` pins
  `4611686018427387905 != 4611686018427387900` and both round to the same `float64` at
  2⁶². And fixing the comparison alone would not have made the arms agree:
  `seekIndexablePreds` marked a served predicate so the scan **skipped** it with no
  residual filter, and the companion index is `float64`-keyed, so above 2⁵³ the seek would
  have started over-returning at the moment the scan stopped under-returning. Six false

  sparse form**. Reachable from the public API with three nodes and two calls, no
  concurrency, no store, no schema. Invisible because the guard already existed in two
  **sibling** functions, so the invariant looked enforced.
- **#2490** — `applySnapshotIndexes` was **dead code**: the `indexes/<name>.bin` payload
  was written on every checkpoint and never read back, because recovery builds its own
  graph and nothing registers on its index manager, so `Result.SnapshotIndexes` was
  **provably always 0** and every index was rebuilt from scratch at every reopen. Every
  test that reached `Deserialize` poked the package-private helper on a hand-built
  manager; the two that went through the real entry points asserted 0. **The obvious fix
  was refuted as unsafe:** registering the index earlier would leave it seekable but
  frozen at the snapshot instant — a silent wrong answer rather than a slow one — because
  there is no index fan-out below `cypher` and `store/txn` does not import `graph/index`
  at all. Hydration is now decided per index, gated on a self-sufficient snapshot, a
  CRC-valid payload and a new additive manifest watermark, with a corrupt payload falling
  back to a rebuild.

## Performance — the honest account

Full detail for this release is in
[`docs/benchmarks/v0.12.0.md`](../docs/benchmarks/v0.12.0.md).

Note the shape of this release before reading the table: it adds no engine capability, so
there is no headline throughput story. The performance work is one large regression
closed, one large regression **accepted for correctness**, two cost floors set from
measurement, and one memory defect that inverted `Compact`'s purpose.

### What got faster

| Axis | Result |
|---|---|
| **Selective multi-label query, default config** (#2431) | **−96.08 %** sec/op, −99.28 % B/op, −98.54 % allocs/op (106 293 → 1 551), p=0.008 — the `v0.11.0` open regression, closed |
| Multi-label, min label above the parallel threshold (#2431) | **−19.92 %** / **−44.16 %** skewed (p=0.008) — the shapes where the parallel scan legitimately wins get *faster* |
| Recovery with hydratable snapshot indexes (#2490) | **−69.65 %** sec/op, −15.88 % B/op |
| `graph/query` integer-bounded numeric range, selective (#2600) | **31.8×** — 44.19 ms → 1.39 ms, B/op and allocs/op identical |
| Indexed integer equality just under the old floor (#2367) | **68.7 µs → 5.5 µs** at 1023 nodes; allocations 889 → ~123 per lookup |
| `count(*)` over a bare typed expansion (#2625) | query **4.52 ms → 2.71 ms** (~40 %) |
| `Compact` after a bracketed 3 M-edge build (#2628) | live heap **361.9 → 159.9 MiB**, matching the unbracketed arm |
| Reverse/undirected relationship binding (#2504) | **2.13 % faster**, allocations identical |

Two of these deserve their caveats stated rather than buried.

**#2431's `-96.08 %` is a repair, not an improvement.** `BenchmarkMinLabelScanSelective_MinLabel`
is now 204.7 µs against `_FirstLabel`'s 3 421 µs — 16.6× faster, the relationship its name
asserts and which did not hold before — and faster than `v0.10.0`'s 230.3 µs, so the
regression is undone rather than merely levelled. **No shape regresses**, and 106 293
allocs/op is exactly the figure the original report quoted, so the attribution is
confirmed on the reported number. The regression gate pins the precedence
**structurally**, comparing plans and never wall clock.

**#2625's scale figure is modest and its variance figure is large.** At 40 000 users over
an interleaved five-run A/B: `count_friend` 8.040 s → 7.504 s (**+6.7 %**) with spread
**52 % → 4 %**, and `count_like` 6.331 s → 6.156 s (**+2.8 %**) with spread **59 % → 9 %**.
Removing seven million per-row allocations takes the GC out of the tail. One early pairing
showed 35 %; five rounds identify it as an outlier in the *before* arm and **it is not
claimed**. The count-store fast path the task prescribed is **refuted and not built**:
`count.Store.CountE` takes no snapshot and a read transaction pins its view, so answering
from the store would violate snapshot isolation.

### What got slower — measured, not minimised

| Axis | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Labelled anonymous-head single-edge pattern** (#2603) | **65× at hub degree 1601** (5.644 µs → 368.8 µs) out-ward, 62× in-ward; **1705× at degree 40 000** (5.681 µs → 9.687 ms), 1660× in-ward. Allocations 193 → 79 566, bytes 8.88 KiB → 627 KiB |
| Snapshot index hydration (#2490) | **allocs/op +24.91 %** — tracked as #2594 |
| `flow.PushRelabel`, one adversarial fan-out shape (#2593) | **+3.3 % to +4.6 %** (p<0.05, three interleaved rounds; B/op and allocs/op identical) |

| Cypher relationship creation (#2444) | **+3.9 %** |

**#2603's cost is the one to read before upgrading if you use anonymous pattern heads.**
The swapped plan is flat in the hub's degree and the written order is linear, so **the
loss grows without bound** on an ordinary spelling. It ships because correctness outranks
speed, and **#2604 is filed to recover the optimisation properly**. A named source
variable is unaffected — the guard declines only sites whose endpoint name is empty.

Three costs are quantified but bounded and do not compound: #2444's cross-checks are the
attributable price of making the node the unit of write–write conflict; #2593's poll is
the price of honouring a deadline, accepted under correct → secure → fast; and #2609's
allocation move brings an `AddRange`-built label to **exactly what an `Add`-built label of
the same ids already cost**, so the change makes two paths do the same work rather than
making either do more. Allocations there are **identical, every sample equal**, for a 100k
dense label, a 100k sparse one and an `Add`-built label of 8 ids.

**One figure was measured, then withdrawn.** A first, *sequential* A/B on #2609 reported a
−8.57 % speedup on the dense label; interleaving **refuted** it (p=0.841), so it was an
artefact and is not claimed.

## Behavioural changes that will surprise

None of these is a breaking API change, and none needs a code change to compile. Each is
something a running system could observe.

- **A query that used to return a wrong answer now returns an error.** `UNION` inside an
  `EXISTS { }` / `COUNT { }` subquery body is refused (#2615), and a relationship
  variable reused across sibling comma patterns of one clause is refused with
  `SyntaxError.RelationshipUniquenessViolation` (#2252). Both previously "worked" and gave
  wrong answers.
- **A query that used to return nothing now returns matches.** `graph/query`'s
  `WithRange` and `WithProperty` unify INTEGER and FLOAT (#2600, #2601), so a float-bounded
  range or a float-valued equality now matches an integer-valued property. If you relied on
  strict kind matching, this changes your result set. One contract widens with it: **a bound
  `float64`-keyed btree covering a `(label, property)` pair must now key every numeric value
  of that property, integers included** — the engine's companion satisfies it by
  construction, but a hand-built float-only index would under-return.
- **Three Bolt status codes changed**, so anything matching on the string will need
  updating: a per-principal quota refusal is now
  `Neo.TransientError.Transaction.MaximumTransactionLimitReached` and leaves the session
  `READY` (#2561); an operator-initiated termination is now
  `Neo.ClientError.Transaction.Terminated` (#2560); and an over-nested parameter is now
  `Neo.ClientError.Statement.ArgumentError` (#2570).
- **A Bolt list is now a PackStream List** (#2513). A client that parsed the stringified
  `"[1, 2, 3]"` form must stop. Correct drivers were already broken by the old behaviour.
- **A failed Bolt `LOGON` closes the connection** (#2556), including a failed
  *re*-authentication, which previously left the connection serving as the previous
  principal. The official `neo4j-go-driver` always sends `LOGOFF` first, so a conforming
  client is unaffected.
- **An unrecognised Bolt `BEGIN` access mode is refused** (#2564) rather than silently
  granting write. The official drivers send exactly `"r"` or `"w"`, so a refusal can only
  reach a client that was previously being granted authority it did not ask for.
- **An autocommit statement's terminal `SUCCESS` carries a fresh bookmark, and one inside
  an explicit transaction carries none** (#2563).
- **`metricTxTimedOut` is narrower** (#2560): it counts the total-lifetime bound alone,
  where it previously also counted idle reaps and operator terminations. Any dashboard
  reading it as "all server-initiated endings" will now under-report; the three counters
  are disjoint and `docs/bolt.md` says so.
- **A schema-validator refusal arrives earlier** (#2602). A `store/txn` write of a
  refused value now fails at `Tx.SetNodeProperty` rather than at `Commit` with
  `ErrCommittedNotApplied`. Callers checking only the `Commit` error will now see the
  refusal at the statement.
- **A snapshot write fails rather than publishing a weightless image** (#2526). A graph
  whose weights the codec cannot encode returns `snapshot.ErrWeightNotPersistable` where
  it previously returned success and lost the weights. **This is the correct behaviour and
  it is a new error path**: supply a codec via `checkpoint.WithWeightCodec` /
  `recovery.Options.WeightCodec` for any non-primitive weight type.
- **`search` entry points now return context errors** they previously swallowed (#2593).
  A caller that passed a cancelled or expired context and used the result anyway will now
  get an error. No live-context result changed.
- **A labelled anonymous-head single-edge pattern gets a much slower — and correct —
  plan** (#2603). See [Performance](#what-got-slower--measured-not-minimised).

## Upgrade notes

```
go get github.com/FlavioCFOliveira/GoGraph@v0.12.0
```

**There are no breaking changes and no migration steps.**

- **No API migration.** No exported identifier was removed, no exported signature
  changed, and no exported struct field was dropped. Existing code compiles unchanged.
- **Dependencies moved, and nothing is required of you.** Every direct dependency was
  raised to its latest release — `roaring/v2` **v2.18.2 → v2.26.0**, `godog`
  **v0.15.1 → v0.16.0**, `klauspost/compress` **v1.18.6 → v1.19.2**, `x/sys`
  **v0.46.0 → v0.47.0** — with the indirect graph following, including
  `gofrs/uuid` being **replaced by `google/uuid`**. `go mod verify` reports all modules
  verified and a module-level CVE scan reports **no vulnerabilities**. Because Go resolves
  the maximum required version across the whole build, a consumer already on newer
  versions is unaffected and one on older versions is raised to these — no API changed in
  any of them, so **no code change is needed either way**. The `go.mod` `toolchain`
  directive moves **`go1.26.5` → `go1.27.0`** while the `go` directive stays at
  **`go 1.26`**, so no new language feature is adopted and the minimum Go a consumer needs
  is unchanged. Note when comparing figures across releases that `v0.11.0` was
  measured on `go1.26.5` and this release on `go1.27.0`, so a cross-release delta mixes a
  compiler change with GoGraph's own.
- **No on-disk migration, in either direction.** The snapshot manifest stays at version
  3 and `csrfile` at version 1. A `v0.11.0` snapshot opens on `v0.12.0`; a `v0.12.0`
  snapshot opens on `v0.11.0`, because an older decoder stops at the manifest's closing
  brace and never reads the new CRC32C trailer. An older `csrfile` reader meeting one of
  the two new weight kinds refuses it with `ErrUnknownWeightKind` rather than misreading
  it. Cross-release opening from `v0.1.0`, `v0.2.0`, `v0.3.0` and `v0.11.0` is now
  exercised under simulation on every run (#2477, #2531).
- **Two things are worth doing on upgrade, neither required.** If you store edge weights
  of any non-primitive type — a struct, or a named integer type such as `time.Duration` —
  **supply a weight codec** (`checkpoint.WithWeightCodec`,
  `bulkimport.PublishWithWeightCodec`, `recovery.Options.WeightCodec`); before #2526 those
  weights were silently discarded at the first checkpoint, and after it the snapshot
  correctly refuses rather than losing them. And if you reopen a `store/txn` store
  yourself, **switch to `recovery.Result.NewStore`** (#2522): every shipped embedder
  previously discarded recovery's derived transaction sequence, so a reopened store
  re-minted sequences that were still live in the WAL.
- **Read [Behavioural changes that will surprise](#behavioural-changes-that-will-surprise)**
  before deploying, particularly if you match on Bolt status codes, parse Bolt list values
  as strings, or rely on `graph/query`'s strict numeric kind matching.

## What this release does not establish

This section is part of the release, not a footnote to it.

1. **This release is not certified.** The most recent production certification is
   [`docs/certification-2026-08-13.md`](../docs/certification-2026-08-13.md), taken at the
   `v0.11.0` release commit — **192 commits behind this tag**. No certification was run in
   this window. Everything the `v0.11.0` envelope did not establish, it still does not.
2. **The whole-tree soak layer was not run** — a fifth consecutive cycle. The artefacts in
   `soak-artefacts/` are from **2026-05-30 at commit `b5453b9`**. The `internal/sim` soak
   layer *was* brought green in this window and verified twice consecutively (#2620), and
   the DST catalogue passes under `SOAK_FULL=1` with 36 of 36 scenarios; neither is the
   whole-tree soak, and neither discharges gate 4 of `1.0.0`.
3. **Latency percentiles at the published concurrency levels were not measured.** p50/p99
   at 1/8/64/256/1024 is unmeasured for a fourth consecutive cycle.
4. **No container was ever run.** The cgroup read paths added under #2421 remain
   unit-tested against injected limits and have never executed: the host is
   `darwin/arm64` and has no cgroup filesystem.
5. **Single host, single architecture** — Apple M4, 10 cores, `darwin/arm64`. No Linux, no
   NUMA, no multi-socket. `GOOS=windows` and `GOOS=linux` were cross-*built* for #2529 and
   #2582; neither was run.
6. **No soak coverage delta is claimed.** #2498 states it plainly: the soak run measured
   71.6 % but exited 1 on a harness defect, and the 55.2 % soak baseline was not
   re-measured.
7. **The DST campaign has no aggregate run tally.** Simulation-run counts, seed counts and
   wall clock are recorded **per task**, not for the campaign; no total exists, and none is
   invented here. The two large parallel swarm exercises of 2026-08-18 and 2026-08-25 were
   run **outside** these sprints.
8. **#2336's torn-total sighting is retired on the absence of evidence, not explained.**
   ~245 M observations are clean and the delta is provably unattributable, but the sighting
   was real, it remains unexplained, and the tail beyond ~245 M was never searched. Its
   acceptance criterion — >1e9 observations per arm — was **not met and is not claimed
   met**.
9. **The 51 new exported identifiers were not exercised by an external consumer.** They
   are covered by the module's own tests and by the DST; no downstream integration exists.
10. **Attribution of #2444's and #2593's costs is per-benchmark, not whole-workload.** Each
    is a measured delta on the shape named, interleaved and with `benchstat`; neither is a
    claim about an end-to-end application.

The honest reading of this cycle is that **the module's gates got substantially harder to
fool, and 50 defects fell out of that** — five of them silent losses of data or wrong
answers that a fully green suite had been reporting as fine. What it is not is a
certification: the instrument improved, and the instrument has still not been pointed at
a soak, a container, a second architecture, or a latency distribution.
